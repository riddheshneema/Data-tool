## Q1. Does Maheshwar have a notified planning area and development plan, is Jalkoti inside it, and who approves layouts outside it?

### Takeaway
Maheshwar has a Development Plan under the 1973 Act with a 2021 horizon. Its implementation was in force from at least 2005, and T&CP amended it in 2020 and 2023. That implies a notified section-13 planning area. No source found says whether Jalkoti (about 6 km from Maheshwar town) falls inside the planning-area boundary. That has to be confirmed at the T&CP district office or from the plan's village schedule. Outside a planning area, colonies in gram panchayat areas are permitted under the MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014. Layouts must still conform to the Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012.

### Inferences
- A development plan under the 1973 Act can only be prepared for a notified planning area (s.13), so Maheshwar has a planning area. Small-town planning areas in MP usually include the nagar parishad plus a ring of adjoining revenue villages. Jalkoti, about 6 km away on the Narmada, may or may not be included. It should not be assumed either way.
- **If Jalkoti is inside the Maheshwar planning area:** the s.16 land-use freeze or the sanctioned plan's land use governs. Riverfront land around Maheshwar is likely zoned agricultural, recreational or heritage. Development permission is needed from the T&CP office under ss.29–30 before the colony permission under the 2014 Rules and diversion. A residential colony on land zoned agricultural would need a plan modification or a use permissible in that zone, such as a farmhouse.
- **If Jalkoti is outside any planning area:** this is a working understanding that needs confirmation. The colony route runs through the 2014 Gram Panchayat colony rules: the competent authority under rule 10 (in practice SDM/Collector-level), diversion under the MP Land Revenue Code, and BVN-conformant layout. T&CP is usually consulted for layout scrutiny. Whether a separate T&CP "approved layout" is legally mandatory outside planning areas is the point to confirm with the T&CP district office.
- Maheshwar's plan horizon (2021) has expired. Under MP practice an expired plan continues until revised. A revised plan, for example a 2035 or 2041 horizon, may be in preparation; none was found.

## Q2. How does T&CP layout / development permission work (sections 29–30): portal, documents, timelines, deemed approval, validity, fees?

### Takeaway
An applicant applies to the Director (district T&CP office) under s.29 and gets a decision under s.30. If no decision is communicated within 60 days, permission is deemed granted (s.30(5)). Permissions remain in force for 3 years (s.33). BVN Rule 23 restricts instant or deemed approval to individual plot holders, not colonisers. Fees from the Indore automated-layout document are Rs 5,000/ha application plus Rs 75,000/ha residential permission. That document is undated, so confirm current rates.

### Cited Findings
- **s.30(5), 1973 Act:** "If the Director does not communicate his decision whether to grant or refuse permission to the applicant within [sixty days] from the date of receipt of his application, such permission shall be deemed to have been granted…". Time taken by the applicant to furnish further documents is excluded — [M.P. Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Adhiniyam 1973, Indian Kanoon (search summary)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/66016430/); [Legitquest Act text](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagar-tatha-gram-nivesh-adhiniyam-1973/1046e)
- **s.33:** "Every permission granted under Section 30 or Section 31 or Section 32 shall remain in force for a period of [three years]" and then lapses — [Indian Kanoon (search summary)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/66016430/)
- Amending Acts listed by T&CP include the Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh (Sanshodhan) Adhiniyam 2019 (17.02.2020) and the 2011 amendment Act. An amendment to the Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Niyam 2012 was made on 28.09.2020 — [mptownplan.gov.in act & rules page (search summary)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/actrules.html); [Legitquest 2011 amendment](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagar-tatha-gram-nivesh-sanshodhan-adhiniyam-2011/102FC)
- **BVN Rule 23:** a s.30 sanction does not lapse in the instant/deemed context. "Only individual plot holder shall be eligible for application of instant/deemed approval. Such permission cannot be issued to the colonizers who intend to sell the plot/flats/building." — [BVN 2012 updated to 6 Mar 2025 (search summary)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf)
- **Online systems:** The T&CP e-Services portal (emptownplan.gov.in) offers online layout permission and states "Online Land Use certificate and Layout permission has been started in Bhopal, Indore, Gwalior and Jabalpur". Khargone is not mentioned — [emptownplan.gov.in (search summary)](https://www.emptownplan.gov.in/)
- **Automated Layout Process Approval & Scrutiny System (ALPASS), Indore:** online application; application fee Rs 5,000 per hectare; permission fee paid via www.mptreasury.org.in at Rs 75,000/ha residential, Rs 1,50,000/ha commercial and Rs 30,000/ha agricultural. The document is undated — [mptownplan.gov.in LU-panel/Indore/alpass.pdf (search summary)](http://mptownplan.gov.in/LU-panel/Indore/alpass.pdf)
- The district T&CP office issues layout permissions. For example, the Joint Director, T&CP, Bhopal–Sehore approved a layout (RERA-uploaded approval). The conditions included that open space "भूमि विकास नियम 2012 के अनुरूप सुनिश्चित किया जावेगा" (to be ensured as per BVN 2012) — [Approval letter, Joint Director T&CP Bhopal–Sehore (RERA document)](https://img.staticmb.com/mbimages/reradoc/2025/01/25/5332653ApprovalTownCountryPlanning_2_2.pdf)
- **Proposed change (not confirmed as notified):** MP plans an overhaul of colony development rules. If the authority does not decide a colony application within the prescribed time, it "will automatically be considered approved after 75 days". Project information would go online — [Indore Talk](https://indoretalk.com/madhya-pradesh-plans-major-overhaul-of-colony-development-rules-2/); also reported as "ढाई महीने में अपने-आप मिलेगी कॉलोनी की मंजूरी" (colony approval automatically within two and a half months) — [The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/state/madhya-pradesh/mp-new-colony-development-rules-online-12013845)
- MP launched an online portal for registration of colonisers — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/online-portal-for-registration-of-colonizers)
- The CAG's Report No. 5 of 2025 (Performance Audit, MP town planning) examines district T&CP layout-permission practice. Its contents could not be read — [CAG Report No. 5 of 2025](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2025/Report-No-05-MP-Town-Planning-English_15-11-2025-signed-(1)-069985b0813fdf7.38091434.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Typical sequence for a colony:**
  1. Land-use certificate from T&CP, if in a planning area.
  2. Layout or development-permission application to the district T&CP office under s.29 (online, where available) with ownership documents (khasra/B-1, naksha), a demarcation/survey plan, contour and site plan, the proposed layout drawn to BVN norms, an access-road proof of at least 7.5 m public road (Rule 38), and fee challans.
  3. T&CP layout approval under s.30, valid 3 years under s.33.
  4. Colony development permission under the 2014 Gram Panchayat Rules (rural) or the Nagar Palika colony rules (urban), including the EWS/shelter obligation and coloniser registration.
  5. Diversion under the MP Land Revenue Code.
  6. RERA registration if the project exceeds 500 sq m or 8 units (central RERA threshold; outside this note's scope).
  7. Development works, then completion.
- **Fee illustration at the ALPASS rates:** 4.047 ha (10 acres) gives about Rs 20,235 application fee plus about Rs 3,03,525 residential permission fee. This is only illustrative.
- The 60-day deemed provision in s.30(5) exists, but BVN Rule 23 bars colonisers from instant or deemed approval. A developer should not rely on deemed approval for a plotted colony.

## Q3. What does MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 require for residential plotted layouts: open space, community facilities, roads, block length, plot sizes, setbacks, FAR, ground coverage?

### Takeaway
BVN 2012 requires the following for a plotted layout:
- 10% of layout area as community open space (Rule 47(2)).
- Roads per Rule 38 Table 3: 7.5 m up to 150 m length, 9 m up to 250 m, 12 m up to 400 m, 18 m up to 1,000 m, 24 m beyond.
- Every plot accessible from a public street at least 7.5 m wide.
- Community facilities scaled to population (Rule 49 / Table 6) rather than a fixed percentage.
- Plot-type minimums under Rule 53, front and rear setbacks under Rule 56, and residential FAR 1.25 (Rule 61 / Table 8).

### Cited Findings
All findings below are from the MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 consolidated PDF (308 pp.), read via document query — [BVN 2012 PDF (citygas.mp.gov.in mirror)](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf):
- **Rule 47(2) (minimum open area):** "Minimum community open space shall be 10 per cent of the area of the layout". EWS/LIG schemes follow Appendix-J, where J-4(2) requires at least 0.2 ha of community open space per 1,000 persons.
- **Rule 38(4)(a), Table 3 (residential means of access):**

  | Road width | Maximum road length |
  |---|---|
  | 7.5 m | 150 m |
  | 9.0 m | 250 m |
  | 12.0 m | 400 m |
  | 18.0 m | 1,000 m |
  | 24.0 m | above 1,000 m |

  Also: "No development on plots or land shall be permitted unless it is accessible by a public street of width not less than 7.5 meters."
- **Rule 43 (cul-de-sacs):** cul-de-sacs of 150–275 m with additional turning space at 150 m are allowed only in residential areas on straight roads. Turning space must be at least 121 sq m with no dimension under 11 m.
- **Rule 49(1), Table 6 (community facilities by population):**
  - Nursery school: 1 per 4,000 persons, 0.1 ha.
  - Primary school: 1 per 4,000 persons, 0.4–0.6 ha depending on density.
  - High school: 1 per 16,000 persons, 2.0 ha.
  - Health centre: 1 per 16,000 persons, 1.0 ha.
  - General hospital: 1 per 80,000 persons, 4.0 ha.
  - Convenience shopping: 10 shops per 4,000 persons, 0.05–0.1 ha.
  - There is no single fixed percentage for public/semi-public land.
- **Rule 53(1)(i) (minimum plot size and frontage):**
  - Detached: above 225 sq m, frontage above 12 m.
  - Semi-detached: 125–225 sq m, frontage 8–12 m.
  - Row-type: 50–225 sq m, frontage 4.5–12 m.
- **Rule 56 (marginal open spaces, residential up to 12.5 m height):**
  - Front, by street width: up to 9 m street, 3.0 m; above 9 to 12 m, 3.6 m; above 12 to 18 m, 4.5 m; above 18 m, 6.0 m.
  - Rear, by plot area: up to 40 sq m, nil; 40–150 sq m, 1.5 m; 150–225 sq m, 2.5 m; above 225 sq m, 3.0 m.
  - Side: detached, 3 m on both sides; semi-detached, 3 m on one side (reducible to 2.5 m); row-type, none.
- **Rule 61 / Table 8:** residential FAR 1.25. Ground coverage for ordinary plotted residential is controlled through the marginal open spaces rather than a stated percentage, per the queried text.
- Appendix-J caps the maximum built-up area of an EWS/LIG dwelling unit at 66 sq m — [BVN updated to Mar 2025 (search summary)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf)

### Inferences
- The consolidation date of the citygas mirror PDF is unknown. Rule numbers and values should be checked against the official consolidation of 6 Mar 2025 and any 2025–26 amendments (see Q6).
- For a 5–20 acre colony (about 2–8 ha), internal loop roads will be mostly 7.5 m and 9 m. A 12 m spine is needed wherever a straight run exceeds 250 m. This effectively limits block lengths, because each road segment's length caps at its width class.
- At typical densities of 4–5 persons per plot and 60–80 plots per ha, a 4 ha colony houses about 1,000–1,600 people. Table 6 then requires at most one nursery or convenience-shop site, so community-facility land is small (about 1–3%).

### Gaps
- The text did not show whether Rule 47(2) was amended upward, for example to 12%, after 2012. Check the 2025–26 amendments.
- No explicit ground-coverage percentage for plotted residential was found in the queried text.
- No explicit "maximum block length" rule beyond the road-length table and the cul-de-sac rule was identified.

## Q4. What do the farmhouse / low-density provisions in agricultural use allow?

### Takeaway
BVN Rule 17(13) permits farmhouses on agricultural land subject to a minimum plot area, FAR 0.10, a 7.5 m height limit with sloping roof, 7.5 m road access, tree planting, and marginal open spaces. Two versions of the trees-per-hectare and setback figures exist in different texts, so the current figures must be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **BVN Rule 17(13), as read from the citygas mirror PDF:**
  - Minimum plot 0.40 ha.
  - Maximum FAR 0.10.
  - Maximum height 7.5 m with sloping roof.
  - At least 250 live trees per hectare before application.
  - Access by a road at least 7.5 m wide.
  - At least 10 m setback on all sides.
  - Source: [BVN 2012 PDF](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)
- **The consolidated BVN (updated to 6 Mar 2025), as summarised by search:** "Maximum F.A.R. shall be 0.10", "a minimum of 100 live trees per hectare", road access at least 7.5 m, and "Minimum marginal open space is 7.5 meters from all sides". The 0.40 ha minimum was not confirmed in that summary — [BVN updated to 6 Mar 2025 (search summary)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/877973186/bvn06032025E)
- **Conflict:** 250 trees/ha with 10 m setbacks in the mirror PDF, against 100 trees/ha with 7.5 m MOS in the 2025 consolidation summary. This may reflect an amendment or summariser error. Flag for verification.

### Inferences
- **Illustration:** a 0.40 ha (4,000 sq m) farmhouse plot at FAR 0.10 allows about 400 sq m of built-up area, single dwelling, roughly G+1 under a sloping roof. A 10-acre site could therefore be subdivided into up to about 10 farmhouse plots of 0.40 ha, if the authority accepts a "farmhouse scheme". Subdivision for sale still triggers the colony rules (registration, EWS/shelter, development permission). Do not treat farmhouse plotting as a way around the colony rules.
- Farmhouse plots need no 10% open space or the full road hierarchy of a residential layout, but they still need 7.5 m road access to each plot.

### Gaps
- The number of dwellings per farmhouse plot, any maximum ground coverage, and whether "farmhouse colonies" are expressly recognised were not found.
- The current (2025–26) wording of Rule 17(13) needs confirmation from the official PDF.

## Q5. What EWS/LIG reservation or shelter fee in lieu applies, including outside planning areas?

### Takeaway
For rural (gram panchayat) colonies, the HC order summary indicates 6% of saleable area reserved for EWS/LIG (3% + 3%), with an off-site option within 2 km. A shelter fund (आश्रय निधि) is collected in panchayat colonies. Townships carry 15% EWS/LIG. A proposed unified colony regime would make 15% EWS/LIG mandatory, but it is not confirmed as notified.

### Cited Findings
- The coloniser must reserve "6% of the saleable area for EWS and LIG (3% each for EWS and 3% for LIG)". For colonies under 2 ha the reservation sits inside the colony; for larger colonies "6% developed plots/dwelling units may be kept reserved for EWS/LIG within the radius 2 kilometre from the main campus". This is in the context of the 2014 Gram Panchayat colony rules — [MPHC WP 31209/2023 order dated 30.05.2024 (search summary)](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf). The rule number was not visible.
- The government collects आश्रय निधि (shelter fund) from colonies in panchayat areas, and district panchayat accounts hold large sums collected since 2014 — [Patrika](https://www.patrika.com/indore-news/modern-colonies-change-the-map-of-mp-district-panchayat-mp-villages-urbanization-fact-report-19673691)
- **BVN:** a FAR exemption applies to "the built up area of such EWS and LIG dwelling units as together constitute 15% of the number of dwelling units proposed in the colony" — [BVN updated to Mar 2025 (search summary)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf)
- **Proposed colony Act / rules:** 15% of area or houses reserved for EWS/LIG, with uniform urban and rural colony rules, reported as a proposal — [Star Samachar](https://starsamachar.com/Madhya-Pradesh:-Government-will-bring-Colony-Act---15-percent-houses-will-be-reserved-for-the-poor); [Indore Talk](https://indoretalk.com/madhya-pradesh-plans-major-overhaul-of-colony-development-rules-2/)
- **Integrated Township Policy 2025:** 15% of total dwelling units for EWS/LIG in townships of 10–40 ha, FAR-free — [Approved Integrated Township Policy 22.02.2025 (PDF)](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Approved-Integrated-Township-Policy-22.02.2025.pdf); [MP Patwari summary](https://mppatwari.com/madhya_pradesh_integrated_township_policy/)

### Inferences
- For a Jalkoti colony under the 2014 rules, plan for 6% of saleable area as EWS/LIG plots, or the applicable shelter fee, if the rule allows payment in lieu. If the proposed 15% regime is notified, the burden more than doubles. Model both scenarios.
- The urban colony rules (MP Nagar Palika (Colony Vikas) Niyam 2021) apply only inside ULB limits, so they apply only if Jalkoti is ever merged into Maheshwar Nagar Parishad.

### Gaps
- The exact rule numbers, current shelter-fee rate per sq m, and whether payment in lieu is allowed under the 2014 GP rules (as amended) were not retrieved from the official text.

## Q6. What changed in 2021–2026: amendments, township policy, small-colony changes, simplified approval?

### Takeaway
Confirmed changes:
- BVN premium FAR (consolidated 6 Mar 2025).
- Green-building bonus FAR (15 Dec 2025).
- Building-permission relief for plots up to 500 sq m.
- MP Integrated Township Policy (approved 22 Feb 2025), followed by MP Integrated Township Rules 2026 (gazette dated 10 Feb 2026; title only seen).

BVN amendments of June 2026 are listed but their contents are unknown. The 75-day deemed colony approval and uniform 15% EWS rules are reported only as proposals.

- **Small-plot relief:** BVN amendment lets registered architects issue building permission for plots up to 300 sq m. For plots up to 500 sq m, permission is given on an affidavit plus fee — [Lokswami (Facebook post)](https://www.facebook.com/lokswamionline/posts/1108772301668810/); [Vindhya Bharata](https://www.facebook.com/vindhya.bharata/posts/1124405403247919/)

- **Integrated Township Policy 2025 (approved 22.02.2025):**
  - Minimum 10 ha in cities under 5 lakh population, 20–40 ha in larger cities.
  - Contiguous land within ULB limits or a planning area.
  - 15% of dwelling units for EWS/LIG.

## Q7. What does a typical land-allocation template look like for a 5–20 acre plotted colony?

### Takeaway
No official worked example was found. Applying the cited norms gives roughly:

| Component | Share of gross area |
|---|---|
| Roads | 22–30% |
| Open space / parks | 10% |
| Community facilities and utilities | 3–6% |
| Gross saleable | 55–63% |
| EWS/LIG, inside saleable (2014 GP rules) | 6% of saleable, or 15% if the proposed regime is notified |
| Net market-saleable | about 52–59% of gross |

### Cited Findings
- Open space at 10% of layout area — [BVN Rule 47(2)](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)
- Road widths of 7.5, 9, 12, 18 and 24 m by length — [BVN Rule 38 Table 3](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)
- Community facilities by population — [BVN Rule 49 Table 6](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)
- EWS/LIG at 6% of saleable area (3% + 3%) — [MPHC WP 31209/2023 (search summary)](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf)
- Township comparator: parks 10% of gross; EWS/LIG 15% of units — [Integrated Township Policy 2025](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Approved-Integrated-Township-Policy-22.02.2025.pdf); [MP Shining](https://mpshining.com/the-mp-government-has-issued-integrated-township-rules-with-80-land-allocated-to-developers-and-mandatory-registration/)

### Inferences
These are worked illustrations by the researcher, not sourced examples.

**10-acre site (40,469 sq m):**

| Component | Share | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Open space (Rule 47(2)) | 10% | 4,047 sq m |
| Roads (7.5–9 m internal, 12 m spine if over 250 m) | about 25% | about 10,100 sq m |
| Utilities: overhead tank or tubewell, transformer, STP or septic, solid-waste point | about 2% | about 800 sq m |
| Convenience shops or nursery site (Table 6) | about 2% | about 800 sq m |
| Gross saleable | about 61% | about 24,700 sq m |
| EWS/LIG at 6% of saleable | | about 1,480 sq m |
| Net market-saleable | about 57% of gross | about 23,200 sq m |

At about 150 sq m average plots (semi-detached band under Rule 53), that is about 155 market plots plus about 10 EWS/LIG plots. If 15% EWS/LIG applies, net market-saleable falls to about 52%.

**5-acre site (about 2 ha):** roads take a higher share (about 28–30%) because of the frontage-to-area ratio. Net market-saleable is about 52–55%.

**20-acre site (about 8 ha):** an 18 m or 12 m spine is likely needed for runs over 400 m. Roads are about 22–25% and net market-saleable about 57–60%. Table 6 may require a primary-school site (0.4–0.6 ha) once the population approaches 4,000.

**Narmada caveat:** a riverfront site may face river-edge no-construction or flood-line restrictions. These could take additional land out of the saleable area. They belong to other researchers' scope but must be layered on.

### Gaps
- No official T&CP-approved Khargone or Maheshwar layout with an area statement was found to validate these percentages.
- The road share depends heavily on site geometry and Narmada frontage. A sketch layout is needed for real numbers.
