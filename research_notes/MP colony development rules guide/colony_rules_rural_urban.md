## Q1. Rural areas (Gram Panchayat): MP Panchayat Raj Avam Gram Swaraj Adhiniyam 1993, ss. 61-A to 61-E, and the Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014

### Takeaway
A colony in a gram panchayat area such as Jalkoti is governed by **ss. 61-A to 61-E of the Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam** and the **MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules, 2014**. The 2014 rules came into force on 24 Dec 2014 and replaced the 1999 colonizer rules. They are still the operative rural rules as of Nov 2026, because the proposed "Integrated" colony law or rules had not been notified (see Q3).

The **competent authority is the SDO (Revenue), i.e. SDM Maheshwar**. Mortgaged plots are held with the **District Collector**. Fees go into the **Zila Panchayat shelter fund**.

### Cited Findings

**Statute and rules (current vs superseded)**
- The MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules, 2014 were notified as No. 627 dated 24 Dec 2014. A draft version (the "2013" draft rules) was published in notification No. 115 dated 1 Mar 2014 — [indianemployees.com gazette listing](https://www.indianemployees.com/gazette-notifications/details/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014); [draft 2013 rules listing](https://www.indianemployees.com/gazette-notifications/details/draft-rules-madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2013)
- The MP High Court held that the 2014 rules "came into force on 24/12/2014" and are not retrospective — [MP HC Indore, WP 31209/2023, order 30 May 2024](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) (via search extract)
- **SUPERSEDED:** The MP Gram Panchayat (Registration of Coloniser, Terms and Conditions) Rules, 1999 were replaced by the 2014 rules — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/); [India Code PDF of 1999 Rules](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_MP_74_277_00001_00001_1541502358911&filename=registration_of_coloniser_terms_and_conditions_rules%2C_1999.pdf&type=rule)
- The 2014 rules implement ss. 61-A to 61-E of the Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam — [Radheshwari Developers v. State of MP, MP HC, 4 Apr 2017](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/)
- MP Real Estate Policy 2019 committed to amending the "M.P. Gram Panchayat (Colony ka Vikas) Niyam, 2014". No resulting amendment notification was found — [MP Real Estate Policy 2019 (mpurban.gov.in)](https://mpurban.gov.in/Uploaded%20Document/guidelines/2-Real_Estate_PolicyF.pdf)

**Key definitions (s. 61-A)**
- **"Gram Panchayat area" (s. 61-A(a))** covers land within these distances:
  - 16 km of the limits of a Municipal Corporation;
  - 8 km of a Municipal Council or Nagar Panchayat;
  - 3 km of other urban areas;
  - 1 km from the side of a National Highway or notified road.

  Source: [casemine – Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam text](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb46894ef23297d8c6c4); [India Code PDF of Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/12801/1/the_chhattisgarh_panchayat_raj_avam_gram_swaraj_adhiniyam,_1993_no._1_of_1994_date_24.01.1994.pdf) (via search extract)
- **"Colonizer" (s. 61-A(c))** means any person, society, institution or entity that intends to develop a colony for transfer by sale or otherwise, other than entities the State notifies as excluded — [MP HC Gwalior WP 15953/2021 (Satyendra Singh)](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2021/WP/15953/WP_15953_2021_FinalOrder_03-Sep-2021.pdf) (via search extract)
- **What counts as establishing a colony:** dividing land into plots, with or without development, for transfer to persons who will build houses or other accommodation. Anyone who does this must first get a registration certificate from the competent authority — [casemine – Act text](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb46894ef23297d8c6c4) (via search extract)
- In Satyendra Singh (WP 15953/2021), the MP HC rejected a reading that would apply s. 61-D only to *registered* colonizers, saying such a reading "would frustrate the very purpose of the Act". Unregistered persons who plot land can therefore be prosecuted — [MP HC WP 15953/2021](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2021/WP/15953/WP_15953_2021_FinalOrder_03-Sep-2021.pdf); [casemine – Satyendra Singh](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/in/635b882eb3d81b18b05e17eb)

**Competent authority**
- The **Sub-Divisional Officer (Revenue)** was appointed Competent Authority by Gazette notification dated 24 Dec 2014 — [Radheshwari Developers v. State of MP (2017)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/) (via search extract)
- Under the 1999 rules, the forms were also addressed to the SDO (Revenue) — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)

**Colonizer registration (rural)**
- Under Rule 3 of the 2014 rules, anyone intending to develop a colony in a Gram Panchayat area applies to the Competent Authority for registration in **Form-1** — [Radheshwari (2017)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/); [MP HC 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf)
- The fees are disputed:
  - **Registration fee ₹50,000; renewal ₹10,000**, both deposited in the shelter fund of the concerned Zila Panchayat — [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) (via search extract).
  - A LinkedIn explainer instead gives **₹5,000 registration / ₹2,000 renewal** without naming the rule set. This probably reflects the superseded 1999 rules — [LinkedIn – Registration of Colonizer and Colonies in MP](https://www-linkedin-com.translate.goog/pulse/registration-colonizer-colonies-madhya-pradesh-harsh-kushwaha?_x_tr_sl=en&_x_tr_tl=hi&_x_tr_hl=hi&_x_tr_pto=tc)
- Under the 1999 rules, a separate registration certificate was not needed for each colony in a "Gram Panchayat Area" — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/) (via search extract)

**Development permission (rural)**
- The development permission fee is **₹10,000 per hectare**, deposited by the colonizer in the **shelter fund of the concerned Zila Panchayat**. The application must attach the fee receipt, and the Competent Authority must reject an application without the fee or with incomplete documents — [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) (via search extract)
- **Timeline:** the Competent Authority must decide the application "within sixty days from the date of presentation". A separate provision deems permission granted if no order is passed within 15 days of a particular reminder letter from the applicant — [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) (via search extract summarised in [FPJ/Indoretalk search result](https://indoretalk.com/madhya-pradesh-plans-major-overhaul-of-colony-development-rules-2/))
- Permission in rural or urban areas is granted by the competent authority **after obtaining the opinion of the Town and Country Planning (T&CP) office** — [MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) (via search extract)
- Development permission is required even for non-residential use — [Radheshwari (2017)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/) (via search summary)
- Under the 1999 rules, establishing a colony, carrying out development work, selling plots or houses, or contracting to sell was valid only after permission was obtained — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)

**Security / mortgage (rural)**
- For plotted, group housing or dwelling-unit colonies, **25% of the total developed plots and/or 25% of dwelling units** must be mortgaged **with the concerned District Collector** — [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/d3b2) (via search extract)
- The 1999 rules also used 25%, but tied the mortgage to the Gram Panchayat — same source

**EWS / shelter fee (rural)**
- The 2014 rules levy a shelter fee. Rule 14(2) bases it on the Collector guideline rate prevailing on the date the colonizer submits the application — [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) (via search extract)
- The colonizer must also pay an **additional shelter fee for maintenance of external roads, drainage and sewage**, and must **hand over external roads, drainage and sewage to the Gram Panchayat**, which then maintains them. A colonizer who allots EWS/LIG plots to ineligible persons can have the registration cancelled and faces penal action — [MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) (via search extract)
- **SUPERSEDED (1999 rules):** every residential colony of one acre or more had to hand over 15% of fully developed plots to the competent authority for EWS — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)
- Patrika reports that the rule to collect *ashray nidhi* (shelter fund) from panchayat-area colonies was made, but no rules were framed for how the money is spent. District panchayats' negligence has therefore stalled development in rural colonies — [Patrika Indore – "Modern colonies changing MP's map"](https://www.patrika.com/indore-news/modern-colonies-change-the-map-of-mp-district-panchayat-mp-villages-urbanization-fact-report-19673691)

**Penalties (rural, s. 61-D)**
- Illegal diversion or illegal colonization, or abetting it: **simple imprisonment up to 6 months, or a minimum fine of ₹10,000, or both**. Building in an area of illegal diversion or colonization ("illegal construction"): up to 6 months, or a minimum fine of ₹1,000, or both. Different text versions show different fine figures, so check the current consolidated text — [indiankanoon – s. 61-D](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48218420/) (via search extract)
- **Forfeiture:** on conviction under s. 61-D(3), the colonizer's right, title and interest in the illegally colonized land stand forfeited to, and vest in, the Gram Panchayat free of encumbrances — [indiankanoon – s. 61-D](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48218420/) (via search extract)
- **Officials:** an official authorised to inspect, stop or remove construction who knowingly fails to act, or a police officer who fails to assist, is also punishable — same source
- In *Shailendra Mudgal v. State of MP* (17 May 2025), a petitioner argued there had been "no amendment in section 61-D to date". This was the petitioner's submission, not a finding of the court — [indiankanoon fragment](https://indiankanoon.org/docfragment/51901474/?big=3&formInput=m.p.+land+revenue+code++++doctypes%3A+madhyapradesh)

### Inferences
- For **Jalkoti**, assuming it lies outside Maheshwar Nagar Parishad limits, the expected sequence is:
  1. Colonizer registration (Form-1) with **SDM Maheshwar**.
  2. **T&CP layout / development permission** from the T&CP office, likely the district office for Khargone or the Indore regional office.
  3. **Diversion** of the agricultural land under the MP Land Revenue Code, before or alongside the colony permission.
  4. Colony development permission from **SDM Maheshwar**, with the fee of ₹10,000 per hectare paid into the Zila Panchayat Khargone shelter fund.
  5. Shelter fee and additional shelter fee.
  6. Mortgage of 25% of plots with **Collector Khargone**.
  7. Development of the colony.
  8. Joint completion inspection with the Gram Panchayat.
  9. Handover to the Gram Panchayat.
- The rural regime is far lighter than the urban one. The rural mortgage is a flat 25% of plots, against 125% of the internal development cost in urban areas. The rural permission fee is ₹10,000 per hectare, against 0.5% of market value in urban areas. Rural penalties under s. 61-D are a maximum of 6 months, against 3–7 years in urban areas. This gap is the stated motive for the proposed "uniform law" (see Q3).
- If Jalkoti lies within **8 km** of Maheshwar Nagar Parishad limits, it falls in the s. 61-A "Gram Panchayat area" belt. Proposed 2026 reforms would bring such fringe villages under urban-style rules (16 km from a Municipal Corporation; the treatment of Nagar Parishads is unclear).

## Q2. Urban areas: MP Nagarpalika (Colony Vikas) Niyam / Colony Development Rules, 2021, and amendments

### Takeaway
The 2021 rules apply in **all Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils and Nagar Parishads** (so they cover Maheshwar Nagar Parishad). The **Collector is the competent authority** for Nagar Parishads and Councils. Registration is **online, statewide, through the Commissioner UAD**.

The main obligations are:
- 0.5% permission fee;
- 2% supervision fee;
- **125% of the internal development cost** secured by mortgaged plots or a bank guarantee;
- 15% EWS/LIG plots or units, or a 5% shelter fee, plus a 1% additional shelter fee;
- 5-year completion deadline;
- deemed transfer to the RWA on completion.

All figures below are from the LegitQuest text dated 13 Jan 2022, read in full.

### Cited Findings
*All in this section: [LegitQuest – MP Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules, 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B), unless stated otherwise.*

- **Source and extent:**
  - Made under ss. 292-A to 292-G of the MP Municipal Corporation Act 1956 and ss. 339-A to 339-G of the MP Municipalities Act 1961. Previously published in MP Gazette (Extraordinary) No. 386 dated 21 Sep 2021; final rules dated 13 Jan 2022.
  - Rule 1(3): the rules apply within all Municipal Corporations, Municipal Councils and Nagar Parishads.
  - Rule 28 repeals the earlier corresponding urban rules and bye-laws, including the old Colonizer Registration rules of 1998. **SUPERSEDED.**
- **Competent authority (Rule 2(1)(e)):** the Municipal Commissioner for a Municipal Corporation; **the Collector for a Municipal Council or Nagar Parishad**.
- **Internal development works (Rule 2(1)(h)):**
  - levelling;
  - demarcation of roads and plots;
  - bituminous or cement-concrete roads (IRC standards);
  - culverts;
  - storm-water drainage (CPHEEO);
  - complete internal water supply;
  - internal sewerage or STP;
  - internal electrification with street lights;
  - development of open spaces (per Rule 47(2) of the Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012);
  - overhead tank, sump or pressure pump;
  - solid waste management;
  - rainwater harvesting;
  - roadside plantation.
- **External development works (Rule 2(1)(g)):** approach road to the existing town road, widening of an existing road, electric line to the colony limit, sewer connection, and water-supply connection. These are generally at the colonizer's cost.
- **Registration (Rules 3–7):**
  - The registration authority is the **Commissioner, Urban Administration and Development** (or a nominee). The process is **online**.
  - Application in Form-One, with the treasury challan and an affidavit on disqualifications. MP Housing Board, Development Authorities, Smart City SPVs and municipalities are exempt from registration, but still need permission and must pay the permission fee.
  - Decision within **30 days**. On rejection, 20% of the fee is deducted from the refund, and an appeal lies to the State within 30 days.
  - Certificate in Form-Two, **valid 5 years**, renewable every 5 years, and **valid across all ULBs in the State**. Registered colonizers are listed on the DUAD website.
  - **Fee: ₹50,000 registration; ₹25,000 renewal** (Rule 4).
  - Disqualifications (Rule 5): false or incomplete information; specified convictions (IPC 153-A, 171-E/F, 505, PCR Act, RP Act s. 125, Dowry Act, etc.); an earlier rejection; unsound mind.
  - Cancellation (Rule 7): false information, or action under s. 292-BA/339-BA or an offence under s. 292-C/339-C. A hearing is required, and an appeal lies to the State within 30 days.
- **Online portal:**
  - Registration was centralised at directorate level in Feb 2022, valid for all ULBs, with a 30-day deadline. Applications run through the **e-Nagarpalika portal, the MP Urban website and the e-service portal**, with online fee payment, document upload, status tracking and SMS/WhatsApp alerts — [ETV Bharat, 23 Feb 2022](https://www.etvbharat.com/hindi/madhya-pradesh/city/bhopal/bhopal-latest-news-mp-colonizers-will-be-able-to-work-in-entire-state-on-single-registration/mp20220223171954787); [Agniban](https://www.agniban.com/the-entire-process-of-colonizer-registration-is-now-online-30-days-deadline-has-also-been-fixed/)
  - Indore Collectorate built its own "CROPS" portal for development permission and colonizer licence applications — [OneIndia Hindi](https://hindi.oneindia.com/news/indore/new-colony-development-work-online-permission-indore-madhya-pradesh-news-1252371.html)
- **Development permission application (Rule 8):**
  - Form-Four, in 5 copies, to the competent authority, **at least 45 days before** starting work or selling plots.
  - Attach proof of the permission fee and the **diversion fee**.
  - **Permission fee = 0.50% of the market value of the diverted land** at Collector guideline rates, deposited in the municipality's treasury.
  - On a revised permission, the fee applies to the revised area only.
  - If the colony is **over 2 hectares**, the colonizer applies in **three phases** on the basis of the T&CP permission and the layout for the whole land.
  - **Rural colony merged into a ULB:** the colonizer re-applies (Form-1 and Form-4), and fees already paid to the Gram Panchayat are adjusted. This is relevant if Jalkoti is later merged into Maheshwar.
- **T&CP NOC and deemed approval (Rule 9):**
  - The competent authority obtains a T&CP NOC. It is not needed if the applicant already encloses a T&CP permission.
  - If departments do not respond within **30 days**, the NOC is deemed given, and the application is disposed of **within 45 days** of receipt.
  - If no decision is communicated, the colonizer gives written notice. If there is still no decision **30 days** after that notice, **permission is deemed granted**, to the extent it complies with the Acts and rules.
  - Rejection requires a hearing and written reasons; an appeal lies to the State within 30 days.
  - A colonizer may surrender the permission before any sale or third-party interest arises (15-day public notice). Fees other than the permission fee are refunded without interest.
- **EWS/LIG (Rules 10–12):**
  - The colonizer chooses one of: **(A) 15% of the total plots** as developed plots for EWS/LIG; **(B) 15% of the residential units** as constructed units; or **(C) the shelter fee**.
  - Shelter fee only is allowed where the EWS plot size is not permissible under the development plan, on leasehold land, and in non-residential colonies.
  - Plots to units in the ratio 3:2.
  - Sizes:

    | Category | Plot | Built unit |
    |---|---|---|
    | EWS | 30–40 m² | 25–35 m² |
    | LIG | 41–96 m² | 36–48 m² |

  - **Shelter fee:** 5% × total plot area × guideline value (plotted); 5% × built-up area × guideline value of buildings (group housing).
  - **Additional shelter fee:** 1%, same base, used for maintenance of external roads, drainage and sewage. It is payable in addition to the shelter fee or reservation.
  - Fees are held in a separate municipal bank account. Building EWS units on-site earns additional FAR equal to the EWS built area.
  - EWS buyers must be residents with no house or plot in MP (affidavit). Prices are capped at 90% of a formula price, with construction cost taken at ₹13,000/m² (independent) or ₹16,000/m² (group housing) for 2021-22, rising 5% a year.
- **Security (Rule 13):**
  - Mortgage plots or flats (excluding EWS/LIG) worth **125% of the internal development cost**, or furnish a **bank guarantee of equal amount**, to the municipality.
  - The bank guarantee must remain valid for 6 months beyond the permitted development period.
  - Mortgaged plots are published and the sub-registrar is notified so that no transfer can be registered.
- **Cost of internal development (Rule 14):** computed using the UDHD Integrated SOR, the Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012, the development plan, DISCOM rates and Collector guidelines.
- **Permission (Rule 15):** no development and no sale before permission. The colonizer pays a **2% supervision fee on the internal development cost**. Permission is issued in Form-Five. The competent authority appoints a qualified engineer to supervise. The colonizer must provide labour welfare facilities.
- **Time limit (Rule 17):** internal development must be completed within **5 years** of permission. One extension of 1 year is allowed for an extra fee of 20% of the Rule 8(2) permission fee. Failure after that leads to action under Rule 20.
  - Patrika Betul reported a gazette amendment extending the internal development period **from 3 to 5 years**. The 5-year figure in the LegitQuest text is therefore the amended position — [Patrika Betul](https://www.patrika.com/betul-news/colonizers-will-get-five-years-permission-for-colony-development-7294257)
- **Release of mortgage (Rule 18):**

  | Works completed | Plots released |
  |---|---|
  | 50% | 50% of mortgaged plots |
  | 75% | a further 25% |
  | 100% (completion certificate) | final 25% |

  At any stage the colonizer may substitute a bank guarantee equal to 125% of the remaining cost. (The Patrika Betul report's "24%" figure for the 75% stage conflicts with the rule text.)
- **Completion (Rule 19):** the colonizer reports completion in Form-Five A. The competent authority has the colony inspected by a technical officer **within 15 days** and issues the completion certificate in Form-Five B. From that date the colony is **deemed transferred to the Resident Welfare Committee (RWA) for maintenance** under the MP Prakoshtha Swamitva Niyam 2019.
- **Violation (Rule 20):** the authority may sell the mortgaged plots or encash the bank guarantee and complete the works itself, recovering any shortfall as arrears of land revenue, after a 30-day notice and a hearing.
- **Rule 21:** lists of permitted colonies are published in newspapers and on the municipality's website.

### Inferences
- If the Jalkoti site, or a future extension of it, is inside **Maheshwar Nagar Parishad** limits, the competent authority for development permission is **Collector Khargone**. In practice this is the Collectorate colony cell, typically run under the Additional Collector or SDM, which is consistent with district practice reported elsewhere.
- Under the urban regime, the colonizer still needs the T&CP layout, diversion and statewide registration.
- Typical urban timeline:
  - registration: about 30 days;
  - development permission: file at least 45 days before starting, decision within 45 days, deemed approval about 30 days after written notice (roughly 75 days in total);
  - works: up to 5 years, extendable by 1 year;
  - completion inspection: 15 days.

## Q3. How the "colony cell" works, and what changed in 2021–2026

### Takeaway
"Colony cell" is an administrative arrangement in Collectorates, not a statutory body. It processes colony permissions where the Collector or SDM is the competent authority (Nagar Parishads and Councils, and rural areas) and acts against illegal colonies.

The main actual changes between 2021 and 2026 were:
- the 2021 urban rules;
- the 2021 penalty hike in the Municipal Acts, to 3–7 years plus a fine of up to ₹10 lakh;
- online, statewide colonizer registration (2022).

As of Nov 2026, a **proposed unified urban-rural regime** (the "MP Colony Integration Act 2026" / "Integrated Colony Development Rules 2026") remained a **draft that had not been enacted**. It proposes 16-km fringe coverage, a single licence, deemed approval at 75 days, and 10-year jail.

### Cited Findings
- **2021 statutory change (urban, current):**
  - The MP Nagarpalik Vidhi (Sanshodhan) Adhiniyam, 2021 (Act No. 22 of 2021, dated 26 Aug 2021) substituted s. 339-C of the Municipalities Act.
  - Illegal colonization, or abetting it, is punishable with imprisonment of **not less than 3 years, up to 7 years, and a fine up to ₹10 lakh**.
  - The competent authority can identify and attach the offender's property, whether it is in the colony or elsewhere.
  - The pre-2021 text (substituted in 2003) provided 3–7 years *or* a minimum fine of ₹10,000, or both.

  Sources: [LegitQuest – Nagarpalik Vidhi (Sanshodhan) Adhiniyam 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalik-vidhi-sanshodhan-adhiniyam-2021/10435); [indiankanoon – s. 339C](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165961955/) (via search extract)
- Amar Ujala reported that the Municipalities Act amendment of 10 Aug 2021 provided for regularisation of illegal colonies — [Amar Ujala](https://www.amarujala.com/madhya-pradesh/mp-news-rules-made-to-legalize-illegal-colonies-residents-of-6-thousand-colonies-of-the-state-will-benefit)
- The 2021 rules provide civic infrastructure and regularisation only for unauthorised colonies that existed **before 31 Dec 2016**. Colonies on government land, development-plan roads or parks, water bodies or highway restricted zones are excluded. Development cost is shared 50:50 (or 20:80 where more than 70% of residents are LIG) — [LegitQuest – 2021 Rules, Rules 23–24](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- Reported deadline: the CM declared about 6,000 illegal colonies legal and extended the legalisation cut-off to 31 Dec 2022. ## Q4. Do farmhouse plots or subdivision of agricultural land count as a "colony"? Risks

### Takeaway
Both rural law (s. 61-A) and urban law define a colony by **dividing land into plots for transfer**, with or without development. **No sources found** give an area threshold or a farmhouse or agricultural exemption.

Selling "farmhouse plots" carved out of agricultural land without registration and permission is exactly the pattern districts are prosecuting. A genuine farmhouse requires at least **0.40 ha per plot** under Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 Rule 17, plus building permission. It is not a route to sell small plots.

### Cited Findings
- **Rural:** establishing a colony means dividing land into plots, *with or without development*, for transfer to persons who will build residences or other accommodation, and it requires registration — [casemine – Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb46894ef23297d8c6c4) (via search extract)
- Section 61-D applies to anyone engaged in illegal colonization, not only registered colonizers — [MP HC WP 15953/2021](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2021/WP/15953/WP_15953_2021_FinalOrder_03-Sep-2021.pdf)
- **Urban:** Rule 26(1) of the 2021 rules makes development of a colony without permission the offence of developing an unauthorised colony, punishable under s. 292-C/339-C — [LegitQuest – 2021 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- **Enforcement in unauthorised colonies (urban Rule 22):**
  - stop-work order and 15-day show-cause notice;
  - a 15-day final notice to remove the construction;
  - intimation to the **sub-registrar to stop registration of sales or agreements**;
  - a public warning in newspapers;
  - demolition and a police complaint against the developer **and the landowner**.

  Source: [LegitQuest – 2021 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- **Rural forfeiture:** on conviction, the colonizer's interest in the illegally colonized land vests in the Gram Panchayat — [indiankanoon – s. 61-D](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48218420/)
- **Farmhouse rules (Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012, Rule 17, consolidated to 6 Mar 2025):** a farmhouse may be permitted in areas designated for agricultural use, subject to:
  - minimum plot **0.40 hectare**;
  - at least **100 live trees per hectare**;
  - access road of at least **7.5 m**;
  - **7.5 m open space on all sides**;
  - maximum FAR **0.10** and maximum height **7.5 m** with a sloping roof (FAR and height from a secondary copy).

  Sources: [MP T&CP – Bhumi Vikas Rules 2012 (updated 6 Mar 2025) PDF](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/877973186/bvn06032025E)
- **District practice:** Bhopal identified 113 illegal colonies on agricultural land for FIR and demolition — [OneIndia](https://hindi.oneindia.com/news/bhopal/bhopal-illegal-colonies-bulldozer-action-113-cases-in-madhya-pradesh-mp-news-1478571.html). Rewa found a 20–30 acre illegal colony on agricultural land — [Rewa Riyasat](https://www.rewariyasat.com/rewa/rewa-illegal-colony-gadariya-collector-fir-action-2026-553013). Dewas booked 21 landowners (more than 26 ha, about ₹500 crore of land) for plotting without approval — [The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/state/madhya-pradesh/mp-action-against-illegal-land-mafia-12218895)
- **Under the 2021 urban rules,** unauthorised colonies created after the cut-off do not get civic infrastructure. In regularised ones, buyers pay 50% of the development cost (or 20% for LIG-majority colonies) — [LegitQuest – 2021 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- **Proposed drafts would penalise buyers who build:** a Patrika headline states that building a house in an illegal colony could attract 10 years' jail — [Patrika Bhopal](https://www.patrika.com/bhopal-news/over-8000-illegal-colonies-in-mp-building-a-house-will-result-in-a-10-year-prison-sentence-20984844). Under current rural s. 61-D, constructing in an illegally colonized area is already "illegal construction", punishable with up to 6 months — [indiankanoon – s. 61-D](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48218420/)

### Inferences
- Selling, say, 0.1–0.2 acre "farmhouse plots" at Jalkoti with internal roads would almost certainly count as a colony. That would expose the seller **and the landowner** to:
  - s. 61-D prosecution (rural) or s. 339-C prosecution (if within Nagar Parishad limits: 3–7 years plus up to ₹10 lakh);
  - forfeiture of the land;
  - a sub-registrar block on registrations;
  - demolition;
  - buyers being denied building permission and utility connections.
- Selling whole agricultural parcels of at least 0.40 ha each as genuine farmhouse or agricultural land, with no common layout or plotting, is lower-risk. It still needs a legal opinion, because no statutory exemption was found and districts judge by substance (layout, roads, marketing as "plots").

### Gaps
- No MP statute or rule was found that expressly exempts agricultural or farmhouse subdivision, or sets a minimum-area threshold for what counts as a "colony". No case law specific to farmhouses was found.
- The ground-coverage limit for farmhouses under Rule 17 was not confirmed.
- Whether the 0.40-ha farmhouse rule applies outside notified planning areas, for example in Jalkoti if it is not in a development plan, needs confirmation from T&CP.

## Q5. Completion and handover: process, inspection, staged release, maintenance

### Takeaway
- **Urban:** report completion in Form-5A, inspection within 15 days, then the completion certificate in Form-5B. Mortgaged plots are released 50% / 25% / 25% at 50% / 75% / 100% completion. On the completion certificate the colony is deemed transferred to the **RWA**.
- **Rural:** report to the SDM and the Gram Panchayat, **joint inspection within 30 days**, completion certificate in Form-7, then release of the 25% mortgaged plots in Form-8. The colony and its external services pass to the **Gram Panchayat** for maintenance.

### Cited Findings
- **Urban Rule 19:** Form-Five A, inspection by a technical officer within 15 days, Form-Five B certificate, deemed transfer to the RWA under the MP Prakoshtha Swamitva Niyam 2019 — [LegitQuest – 2021 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- **Urban Rule 18:** phased release 50/25/25. A bank guarantee equal to 125% of the remaining cost may substitute at any stage — same source
- **Urban Rule 13(3):** building permission may be granted on mortgaged plots. Rule 16: building permission may be granted after development permission — same source
- **Rural 2014:**
  - the colonizer reports completion to the Competent Authority and the Gram Panchayat;
  - the Competent Authority inspects **jointly with Gram Panchayat representatives within 30 days**;
  - if the works and EWS/LIG allotments are verified, it issues the completion certificate in **Form-7** and releases the mortgaged property by order in **Form-8**;
  - for phased permissions, completion certificates are issued phase-wise.

  Source: [LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/d3b2) (via search extract)
- **Rural handover:** the colonizer hands over external roads, drainage and sewage to the Gram Panchayat, which maintains them, funded by the additional shelter fee — [MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf)
- **SUPERSEDED 1999 rules:** the colony was deemed transferred to the Gram Panchayat for maintenance on the date of the certificate, and the certificate was due within 15 days of the completion notice — [indiankanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)
- **Proposed 2026 law:** deemed permission where completion certificates are delayed — [FPJ](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/noose-on-illegal-colonies-to-tighten-as-uniform-law-to-crack-down-for-16-km-radius-from-municipal-limits)

### Inferences
- At Jalkoti, the rural mortgage is not staged by percentage of works in the sources found. If that holds, the colonizer may have to finish all works, or each phase where permission was phased, before the 25% of plots is released. The urban staged-release model would not apply unless the integrated law is adopted.

### Gaps
- Not found: a staged or partial release mechanism under the rural 2014 rules; who maintains internal roads, parks and street lights after rural completion (Gram Panchayat vs residents' society); and the rural time limit for works.

## Q6. Fees, charges and penalties (consolidated)

### Takeaway
Urban costs scale with land value: 0.5% permission fee, 2% supervision fee, 5% shelter fee plus 1% additional, and 125% security. Rural costs are low and flat: ₹10,000 per hectare permission fee, ₹50,000 registration (disputed), shelter fee, and 25% of plots as mortgage. Criminal exposure is 3–7 years plus up to ₹10 lakh in urban areas, against up to 6 months plus a minimum fine of ₹10,000 in rural areas, with steeper penalties proposed.
