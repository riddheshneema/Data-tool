# Utilities, Riverbank, Heritage and Environmental Requirements for a Narmada-bank Colony at Jalkoti (Maheshwar tehsil, Khargone, MP)

Research note, compiled 12 Nov 2026. Research constraint: the Firecrawl credit allowance ran out partway through, and direct fetches of indiankanoon.org, mptownplan.gov.in, indiaenvironmentportal.org.in and supremetoday.ai were blocked by this session's network policy. Many findings below therefore rest on search-result excerpts rather than full documents, and are flagged as such. Two primary documents were read directly: the MPERC Electricity Supply Code (mperc.in PDF) and the TOI report on the 300 m Narmada ban.

## Electricity: MP West Discom / MPERC rules for colony electrification

### Takeaway
Under the MP Electricity Supply Code, the developer of a housing colony pays for the whole extension: the 11 kV line, distribution transformer(s) and LT network. Above 2,150 kW of colony load, or where the existing 33/11 kV substation cannot be augmented, the developer also pays for the 33 kV line and the 33/11 kV substation. The developer may build the works through an MP-licensed 'A' class contractor using discom-approved estimates, paying the discom a supervision charge. I could not find a current published per-kW or per-plot rate for MPPKVVCL (West Discom, Indore). That figure has to be taken from the discom's published notice or obtained from the local O&M circle (Khargone/Mandleshwar).

### Cited Findings
- Supply Code cl. 4.41 (verbatim): "The developer/ builder/ society/ consumers of a housing colony shall bear the cost of extension including the cost of 11 kV line, distribution transformer and LT lines/ LT cables. The developer/ builder shall also pay 'System Strengthening Charges'/ 'Capacity Building Charges', if any." — [MPERC, MP Electricity Supply Code PDF](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Cl. 4.41 continues: "In case it is not possible to give supply to a housing colony by augmentation of the capacity of existing 33/11 kV substation or the load of the housing colony exceeds 2150 kW, the developer/ builder/ society/ consumer shall bear the cost of 33 kV line and 33/11 kV substation of required capacity." The developer definition covers "any agency whether Government, local body or private". — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Cl. 4.42 sets the deemed load per plot used to size infrastructure and charges (by plot area): up to 500 sq ft = 1 kW; 500–1,000 = 2 kW; 1,000–1,500 = 3 kW; 1,500–2,000 = 4 kW; 2,000–2,400 = 5 kW; 2,400–3,000 = 7 kW; 3,000–3,500 = 10 kW; EWS plot = 1 kW; informal sector = 0.5 kW. Common-facility loads (water pump, street lights, etc.) are taken as declared by the developer. If houses are later built for sale instead of plots, the load is reassessed (cl. 4.39) and the developer pays the difference plus any extra infrastructure. — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Cl. 4.42 also says the deemed load is used for "deciding the number and capacity of distribution transformers and the length of HT/ LT line required". Individual service connection charges and security deposits are based on the load each consumer declares at connection time. — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Cl. 4.43: on a requisition for supply to a housing colony, the licensee follows the procedure in cl. 4.19–4.28 (application, estimate, execution). Cl. 4.28 ties completion to the timeframe in cl. 4.74(1). — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Cl. 4.11 lets the consumer build the works themselves. HT/EHT line, substation and LT line extension go through an 'A' class licensed contractor and the service line through a 'C' or higher-class contractor, all "as per the estimates and layout approved by the licensee". The consumer procures the materials and pays "supervision charges as approved by the Commission in the Schedule of Miscellaneous Charges". — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- A non-official Indore city guide gives the supervision charge as 5% when the applicant uses an A-class contractor. Treat this as unverified. — [Indore Online city guide](https://deals.indoreonline.in/city-guide/electricity-in-indore)
- Multi-consumer complex rule (cl. 4.29–4.30): a building or group of buildings with more than one connection and total load of 50 kW or more is a multi-consumer complex. It must be supplied through a separate distribution transformer of at least 100 kVA. — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- Street lighting (cl. 4.52–4.53): the licensee provides the meter/switch box and maintains street-light poles and lines "on payment basis". Fixtures and bulbs are supplied by the consumer. — [MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)
- MPERC (Recovery of Expenses and other Charges for providing Electric Line or Plant ...) (Revision-II) Regulations, 2022 (Gazette 31 May 2022): "The cost of electrification (LT line and Transformer only) per kW based on above estimates shall be declared and published in Newspapers by the Distribution Licensee". These are the regulations behind any published per-kW electrification rate. — [MPERC Gazette 31 May 2022](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64140ba9cef8abeaa895ae894404f764.pdf); [LegitQuest summary](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-electricity-regulatory-commission-recovery-of-expenses-and-other-charges-for-providing-electric-line-or-plant-used-for-the-purpose-of-giving-supply-revision-ii-regulations-2022/C687)
- The Supply Code is "Madhya Pradesh Electricity Supply Code, 2021 as amended from time to time". A sixth amendment allows self-declaration in place of a contractor test report for eligible LT consumers up to 150 kW. — [MPERC Gazette 2022](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64140ba9cef8abeaa895ae894404f764.pdf); [PowerPeak Digest](https://powerpeakdigest.com/mperc-supply-code-self-declaration-150kw-lt/)

### Inferences
- Worked example: 50 plots of 1,500–2,000 sq ft give 50 × 4 kW = 200 kW, plus declared common load (borewell pump, street lights). At typical diversity this probably means one or two 200–250 kVA transformers on an 11 kV tap from the nearest feeder. That is well below the 2,150 kW threshold, so no 33/11 kV substation should be needed unless the local substation has no spare capacity. Transformer sizing and diversity factor are my inference, not discom figures.
- Practical sequence: (1) apply to the MPPKVVCL division/circle (Maheshwar/Mandleshwar O&M division, Khargone circle) with the approved layout; (2) get the feasibility, estimate and load-sanction; (3) either deposit the full estimate (discom executes) or deposit the supervision charge and have an A-class contractor build to discom specification; (4) get discom inspection and hand over the 11 kV/transformer assets to the discom, which then serves individual plot connections under ordinary rules.
- A farmhouse colony in a rural area may sit on a mixed-use or agricultural (AP) feeder with rationed rural hours. Ask the discom for 24-hour supply from a non-agricultural (domestic/"gaon") feeder, which may require a separate feeder segregation estimate.

### Gaps
- Current MPPKVVCL published "cost of electrification per kW" newspaper notice (2025–26) not found. Obtain it from mpwz.co.in or the circle office.
- Exact current supervision-charge percentage in MPERC's Schedule of Miscellaneous Charges not verified (the 5% figure is from a non-official source).
- Whether the 2021 Supply Code's clause numbers (4.41–4.43) were renumbered by later amendments (the PDF read appears to be the consolidated code, but its exact amendment date was not confirmed).
- Whether MP colony rules require the colonizer to deposit electrification cost or a bank guarantee with the Gram Panchayat/SDM before the development permission. This is likely covered under the colony-rules research stream.

## Water and sanitation: colony rule requirements, PHED/Gram Panchayat, groundwater NOC

### Takeaway
A colony in a Gram Panchayat area falls under the MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules, 2014. These list water pipelines, drainage, sewerage and open-space development among the colonizer's mandatory internal and external development works. The urban MP Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules, 2021 are reported to be due for extension to rural areas through a draft "Integrated Colony Development Rules 2026" (status unconfirmed). On groundwater, the CGWA 2020 guidelines require an NOC for residential apartments and group housing societies, with exemptions mainly for individual households and rural drinking-water use. A small plotted colony relying on borewells should confirm the block's CGWA category and whether an NOC is required.

### Cited Findings
- The MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules, 2014 were notified on 24 Dec 2014 under the MP Panchayat Raj Avam Gram Swaraj Adhiniyam, 1993 and "shall be applicable to the Gram Panchayat areas of Madhya Pradesh". — [LegitQuest text of 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2)
- The 2014 Rules define external development to include new and widened connecting roads, and list "development of proposed open areas in the colony" as internal development work. The search excerpt gives "a minimum of 3.75 meters carriage way and 6 meter road way for a colony less than 2 hectares in area". This comes from a search summary, not the rule text read directly. — [LegitQuest 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2)
- A 2026 MP High Court matter refers to "external development works like laying water pipelines etc, as mandated under Rule 12" of the colony rules. — [LiveLaw PDF, Bharat Enterprises v State of MP (May 2026)](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/05/12/bharat-enterprises-v-state-of-madhya-pradesh-673503.pdf)
- The 2014 Gazette (15 July 2014 notification) set the EWS income criterion at Rs 1,00,000 per family per year. — [LegitQuest 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2)
- The MP Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules, 2021 (Gazette Extraordinary No. 386, 21 Sept 2021) apply to municipal areas only. — [LegitQuest Nagarpalika Colony Rules 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- Patrika (9 Apr 2026) reports that the Urban Colony Development Rules 2021 "will soon be extended to Gram Panchayat areas" and that a draft "Integrated Colony Development Rules 2026" has been prepared. It also reports a crackdown under which FIRs against illegal colonies in villages must be registered within 15 days. — [Patrika, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.patrika.com/en/bhopal-news/strict-action-illegal-colonies-villages-fir-to-registered-within-15-days-20487676)
- CGWA revised groundwater guidelines notified 24 Sept 2020, with amendments dated 29 March 2023 and a later SOP. — [CGWA consolidated guidelines](https://cgwa-noc.gov.in/landingpage/Guidlines/ConsolidateGuidline.pdf); [Maharashtra PCB copy of 2020 notification](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/water-quality/standards-protocols/Ground_Water_NewGuidelinesNotifiedeng24092021102020.pdf)
- Under the CGWA guidelines (as summarised in an NGT reply), residential apartments and group housing societies need an NOC for domestic use in urban areas where the government supply agency cannot meet demand. Installing an STP "is mandatory for all new residential apartments/Group Housing Societies where ground water requirement is more than 20 KLD". Urban residential apartments and group housing pay groundwater abstraction charges. Where State provisions are more stringent, they prevail. — [CGWA guidelines](https://cgwa-noc.gov.in/landingpage/Guidlines/ConsolidateGuidline.pdf); [NGT reply OA 06/2021](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Reply%20by%20R-4%20in%20OA%20No.06-2021%20(page%20nos.959-963).pdf)
- The Ministry exempted farmers (agricultural use) and individual domestic consumers from needing a groundwater NOC under the 2020 guidelines. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/farmers-exempted-from-taking-noc-to-extract-groundwater-893414)
- MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012: a consolidated English text current to March 2025 is on the T&CP site. Amendments were notified in late 2025, and a further draft amendment was published in 2026. The rule text could not be fetched from this session. — [MP T&CP consolidated BVN (Mar 2025)](https://mptownplan.gov.in/act%20&%20Rules/bvn06032025E.pdf); [TeamLease: BVN amendments](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50844/govt-of-madhya-pradesh-issued-amendments-to-the-madhya-pradesh-bhumi-vikas-niyam-2012/); [TeamLease: draft BVN amendment](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/57899/draft-amendment-to-madhya-pradesh-bhumi-vikas-niyam-2012/)

### Inferences
- For a Gram Panchayat colony, the developer should expect to provide and bear the cost of: an internal water distribution network with a source (borewell/tubewell) and a storage tank (overhead or sump), storm-water drains, sewerage, internal roads, street lights and developed open space. These are typically secured by mortgaging a share of plots or by bank guarantee until completion. The mortgage/guarantee share and the exact service specifications are in the 2014 Rules and were not read here.
- A Narmada-bank site needs a sewage solution that discharges nothing untreated to the river. The NGT's 2025 Narmada orders target untreated discharge (see riverbank section). For a small colony, a decentralised STP or septic tank plus soak pit set back from the bank is the likely expectation, and the Gram Panchayat, MPPCB or Collector may insist on an STP for larger plotted schemes. This is inference; confirm with the Khargone town planning office and the MPPCB regional office (Indore/Khargone).
- Groundwater: Maheshwar block's CGWA category (safe, semi-critical, critical or over-exploited) determines how hard the NOC is to get. A plotted colony where each owner drills a household borewell may fall under the individual-domestic exemption. A common colony borewell supplying many plots probably needs the colonizer to apply on cgwa-noc.gov.in or to the MP state groundwater authority, if MP has notified one.

### Gaps
- Exact water-supply standard (lpcd), overhead-tank capacity, rainwater harvesting thresholds and STP plot-area thresholds in MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 (as amended 2025) were not verified because mptownplan.gov.in was unreachable. My recollection is that BVN mandates rainwater harvesting for plots above about 140 sq m, but this is unverified and must be checked.
- Whether Bhumi Vikas Niyam applies to a village outside any notified planning area (Maheshwar has a development plan; whether Jalkoti falls within the Maheshwar planning area is unknown).
- Maheshwar block's CGWA groundwater category, and whether MP has its own groundwater regulation that supersedes CGWA for the state.
- PHED (Public Health Engineering Department) or Jal Jeevan Mission tap-connection role for private colonies: no source found.
- Solid-waste requirements for rural colonies (SWM Rules 2016 apply to gated communities above certain sizes in urban areas; rural applicability unconfirmed).

## Narmada riverbank restrictions: 300 m rule, HFL, NGT floodplain orders, Maheshwar dam

### Takeaway
This is the most serious constraint on the site. The MP government has told the MP High Court that construction within 300 m of the Narmada banks is banned under a Town & Country Planning notification. The High Court has ordered removal of structures within 300 m, with at least one bench measuring the 300 m from the high flood level (HFL). In September 2025 the NGT directed MP to demarcate Narmada floodplains as no-development zones (reportedly on a 25-year flood basis) and to remove encroachments. Until a site-specific HFL/floodplain demarcation and written NOCs are obtained, a riverbank colony at Jalkoti should be planned so that all plots and buildings sit beyond 300 m from the HFL.

### Cited Findings
- TOI (c. Sept 2022; date inferred from article ID and the bench of CJ Ravi Malimath and Justice Vishal Mishra): the state government, answering a PIL by Dayoday Seva Kendra, "said that construction up to 300 metre from the banks of Narmada is banned. Referring to a notification of the town & country planning department, the state government said there can be no construction up to a distance of 300 metre from the banks of river Narmada." It adds: "A division bench of MP high court had ordered for removal of all the structures that have come up within a distance of 300 metre from the banks of river Narmada," with filming of removals and an amicus curiae appointed. — [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bhopal/constructions-upto-300-metre-from-narmada-bank-banned/articleshow/94442983.cms)
- A Tilwaraghat (Jabalpur) report says the High Court "had made it clear that all the construction done at the state level within 300 meters of high flood level on the banks of Narmada" must be identified, and called for a status report. — [India Legal](https://indialegallive.com/top-news-of-the-day/news/narmada-bank-encroachment-jabalpur-tilwaraghat/)
- A judgment on casemine (Narmada Mission matter) records a prayer or direction to "prohibit any construction within area of 300 meters on both sides of the banks of river Narmada except the ones that are specifically exempted." — [Casemine judgment](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/in/67c3753c179d826378c1e5ce)
- Samayak Jain v State of MP (9 Oct, year not visible in excerpt): municipal corporations of towns along the Narmada are to carry out an exercise on encroachments and unauthorised constructions. — [Indian Kanoon (excerpt only)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/188255021/)
- Search summaries report that a High Court division bench gave the state time to frame rules or guidelines for construction near the Narmada in urban and rural areas, and that a petitioner argued the master plan "categorically prohibited any kind of construction activity within 300 metres of the river banks". Dates were not visible. — [India Legal](https://www.indialegallive.com/?p=127254)
- NGT order of 9 Apr 2015 on "construction near Narmada river with respect to no construction zone and the 300 meter zone" (title only; full text not retrieved). — [India Environment Portal](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/in-court/order-of-the-national-green-tribunal-regarding-construction-near-narmada-river-with-respect-to-no-construction-zone-and-the-300-meter-zone-09042015)
- NGT order of 1 Sept 2025 (Kirti Kumar Sadashiv Bhatt v Narmada Water Resources & Ors), per legal digests: complete halt to discharge of untreated sewage; "immediate demarcation of river floodplains as 'no-development zones'" using a 25-year flood cycle; removal of all encroachments; a Chief Secretary-monitored committee meeting quarterly. About 51 of roughly 200 MLD of sewage from 14 major Narmada towns in MP goes untreated into the river. — [SupremeToday digest](https://supremetoday.ai/ngt-issues-sweeping-directives-to-stop-narmada-river-pollution-mandates-demarcation-of-no-development-floodplain-zones-INDNGT00000029736); [Dainik Jagran English](https://english.dainikjagranmpcg.com/states/madhya-pradesh/ngt-directs-madhya-pradesh-government-to-act-against-narmada-pollution/article-13384)
- The NGT also ordered a complete ban on plastic use within 100 m of the Narmada, together with floodplain demarcation and removal of constructions. — [Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/local/mp/news/ngt-plastic-ban-100m-narmada-river-pollution-action-report-137085816.html)
- NGT follow-up, 29 Jan 2026: reiterated strict compliance with the Narmada rejuvenation orders and directed quarterly reviews by MP's environment department. — [Down To Earth court digest, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/daily-court-digest-major-environment-orders-january-29-2026)
- An NGT committee report in OA 109/2025 recorded "progressive encroachment by means of concrete construction within the Flood Plain Zone of the River Narmada" and directed authorities to halt it. — [NGT OA 109/2025 committee report](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Final%20Report%20of%20Committee%20in%20OA%20No.109-2025%20(page%20nos.329-371).pdf)
- MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 setbacks cited in a High Court order: 9 m from water bodies and 15 m from rivers. These are general minimums, much smaller than the Narmada-specific 300 m. — [MP HC WA 318/2018 final order, 5 Apr 2023](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/jabalpur/MPHCJB/2018/WA/318/WA_318_2018_FinalOrder_05-Apr-2023.pdf)
- Maheshwar Hydel Project (400 MW): the reservoir was allowed to fill to 154 m in 2012 (NGT first stayed, then permitted it). A committee found water at 154.3–155.5 m submerged houses in six villages. The project would affect 61 villages (21 fully submerged, 40 losing farmland). A PFC insolvency application against the developer was admitted in NCLT in Sept 2022, and the project is described as effectively abandoned. — [India Environment Portal](https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/ministry-pours-cold-water-on-filling-maheshwar-reservoir); [Land Conflict Watch](https://www.landconflictwatch.org/conflicts/maheshwar-hydropower-project); [CEA quarterly report](https://cea.nic.in/wp-content/uploads/he___td/2025/11/QPR_July_sept_2025_Final.pdf); [Countercurrents, Jul 2025](https://countercurrents.org/2025/07/maheshwar-dam-on-the-narmada-will-have-to-be-abandoned-testimony-to-improper-river-management-in-india/)

### Inferences
- Treat 300 m from the HFL (not from the low-water edge) as the working no-construction line. The High Court has used HFL as the reference in at least one matter, and HFL-based measurement is the conservative reading. On the flat Nimar banks, HFL can lie well inland of the normal channel.
- Superimpose the NGT 2025 floodplain no-development zone (25-year flood) once it is demarcated. Ask the Water Resources Department or NVDA for the site's HFL and floodplain line, in writing.
- Maheshwar dam: even though the project is stalled, land acquired for or marked within the submergence zone (Full Reservoir Level about 162 m, from my recollection, unverified; the filling permission was to 154 m) may be legally encumbered. Check the khasra against NVDA or Collector submergence lists and the land's revenue record for any acquisition entry.
- Agricultural land within 300 m may still be usable for farming or open uses. Whether temporary farmhouse structures within 300 m are allowed is unclear and, given HC removal orders, high-risk.

- Whether a "Narmada Seva Mission" or state cabinet decision formally codified the 300 m rule or set exemptions. Not found.
- Primary text of the NGT 1 Sept 2025 order. Its details come from digests that disagree slightly on dates.
- Maheshwar project FRL and whether Jalkoti appears in the submergence list. Not found. (Search engines confused Sahastradhara with Dehradun's Sahastradhara. Maheshwar's Sahastradhara is a separate rapids site on the Narmada near Jalkoti; that location is local knowledge to be confirmed.)

## Heritage: ASI / State Archaeology restrictions in Maheshwar; Ahilya Lok

### Takeaway
Searches of the ASI Bhopal Circle lists found centrally protected monuments in Khargone district only at Un (Chaubara Dera, Jain temples 1–3, Mahakaleshwar and Nilkanteshwar temples). No ASI-protected entries were found for Maheshwar, Mandleshwar or Jalkoti, so the AMASR Act 100 m prohibited and 300 m regulated zones probably do not bite at Jalkoti. State-protected status of Maheshwar's fort, ghats and temples was not confirmed and must be checked with the MP Directorate of Archaeology. The Ahilya Lok heritage project (about 15 acres, about Rs 110 crore, MP Tourism) is on Maheshwar's riverfront and will drive land demand and possibly local regulation.

### Cited Findings
- The ASI Centrally Protected Monuments list for Khargone includes "Chaubara Dera, Khargone" and "Jain temple 1 to 3". The ASI Bhopal Circle pages place these at Un, Khargone, together with the Mahakaleshwar 1 and 2 and Nilkanteshwar temples (11th-century Paramara, Bhumija style). — [ASI CPM list](https://asi.nic.in/pdf/CPM_List.pdf); [ASI Bhopal: Chaubara Dera](https://asibhopal.nic.in/monument/khargone_un_chaubaradera.html); [ASI Bhopal: Jain temples 1–3](https://asibhopal.nic.in/monument/khargone_un_jaintemple1to3.html); [ASI Bhopal monument list](https://asibhopal.nic.in/monument/all_monument4.html)
- The NMA list of centrally protected monuments (MP, Bhopal circle) shows "Chaubara Dera, Khargone" as entry 1591. — [NMA list](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=1&level=1&ls_id=965&lid=1276&nma_type=0)
- State-protected monuments in MP are governed by the MP Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1964, administered by the Directorate of Archaeology, Archives and Museums. The state list runs to several hundred entries (326 to over 500, depending on edition). — [Wikipedia: State Protected Monuments in MP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_State_Protected_Monuments_in_Madhya_Pradesh); [CAG Report No. 7 of 2022 (MP)](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2022/Report-No.-7,-2022,-Madhya-Pradesh-068b146a359d707.32542777.pdf)
- Ahilya Lok: a cultural and heritage site on Maheshwar's Narmada riverfront honouring Ahilyabai Holkar, on about 15 acres of government land at about Rs 110 crore. MP State Tourism Development Corporation tendered Rs 36.94 crore for phase 1, in three phases over a 22-month timeline, modelled on Ujjain's Mahakal Lok. Groundbreaking status is unconfirmed. — [Drishti IAS, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/ahilya-lok-project); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/ahilya-lok-development-project-to-transform-maheshwar); [Chronicle India, Feb 2026](https://www.chronicleindia.in/online-magazine/archive-csce-february-2026/ahilya-lok-project)
- Maheshwar received development funds of about Rs 1,551 crore, announced around the MP cabinet meeting held at Maheshwar for Ahilyabai's 300th birth anniversary (2025). The Maheshwar–Janapav lift irrigation project (123 villages) was renamed after her. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/mps-maheshwar-receives-development-funds-worth-1551-crore); [Daily Pioneer](https://dailypioneer.com/2025/state-editions/maheshwar-janapav-lift-irrigation-project-named-after-ahilyabai.html)

### Inferences
- AMASR Act, 1958 as amended in 2010: 100 m around a centrally protected monument is prohibited and a further 200 m beyond it (300 m total) is regulated, needing NMA/competent-authority permission. This is from statutory knowledge; NMA is the regulator ([nma.gov.in](https://www.nma.gov.in/)). Un is roughly 50+ km from Maheshwar by my estimate, so Jalkoti is very likely outside any central heritage zone.
- MP's state Act has analogous protected and regulated areas. If any Maheshwar fort/ghat structure is state-protected and Jalkoti lies within a few hundred metres of it, permission from the Directorate is required. Jalkoti is upstream or outside the main fort-ghat complex (exact distance unverified), so this is likely low risk but must be checked.
- No eco-sensitive zone (ESZ) is known around Jalkoti. The nearest notified protected areas are not near Maheshwar, but no source was found confirming this.

### Gaps
- Definitive check of the MP state-protected list for Maheshwar (Ahilya Fort/Rajwada, ghats, Kaleshwar and other temples). Not confirmed.

## Environmental clearance (EIA 2006) and MPPCB consent

### Takeaway
Under the EIA Notification 2006, building and construction projects need prior Environmental Clearance from SEIAA-MP (Category B) only when built-up area is 20,000 sq m or more (item 8(a)). Townships and area development need it when they cover 50 ha or more, or exceed 1,50,000 sq m built-up (item 8(b), Category B1). A small plotted farmhouse colony, say under 10–20 ha with only roads and services built by the developer, would normally fall below both thresholds. MPPCB consent to establish and operate (Water and Air Acts) is still commonly required for residential projects with significant built-up area or sewage generation, but no official MP threshold was confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Item 8(a) of the EIA Notification 2006 schedule: building and construction projects of 20,000 sq m and above but below 1,50,000 sq m built-up area need EC. Built-up area means covered construction; for facilities open to the sky, it is the activity area. — [NGT counter affidavit OA 69/2024 (MoEFCC)](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Affidavit%20by%20R-1%20in%20OA%20No.69-2024%20(page%20nos.361-422).pdf); [Supreme Court, Vanashakti v Union of India](https://www.supremecourtcases.com/vanashakti-v-union-of-india-4/amp/)
- Item 8(b), townships and area development: covering an area of 50 ha or more and/or built-up area of 1,50,000 sq m or more. All 8(b) projects are appraised as Category B1. Both 8(a) and 8(b) are Category B, appraised by SEAC and approved by SEIAA. — [NGT affidavit OA 36/2024](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Counter%20Affidavit%20by%20R-1%20in%20OA%20No.36-2024%20(page%20nos.994-1047).pdf); [Supreme Court, Vanashakti](https://www.supremecourtcases.com/vanashakti-v-union-of-india-4/amp/)
- The EIA notification has been amended several times, so check the current gazette text. A state board clarification (Punjab PCB) describes an exemption for projects under 50 ha where no single building reaches 20,000 sq m. This is a Punjab interpretation, not MP. — [Corpseed: PPCB clarification](https://www.corpseed.com/law-update/ppcb-clarifies-environmental-clearance-rules-for-eia-item-8a-and-8b-projects)
- White-category industries are exempt from consent to operate. A residential project is not a white-category industry. Consent practice for residential projects (as seen in Maharashtra PCB committee minutes) treats them through consent to establish based on plot and built-up area. — [MPCB revised categorisation](https://mpcb.gov.in/consentmgt/revised-industry-categorization); [MPCB consent committee minutes 2022-23](https://www.mpcb.gov.in/sites/default/files/cc-meetings/minutes/34th%20CC%20of%202022-23-Final%20MoM.pdf)
- Note: the "MPCB" minutes found are the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board's, not Madhya Pradesh's (MPPCB). They show general practice only. — same sources as above
- A third-party guide describes the CTE/CTO/CCA procedure in Madhya Pradesh, run through MPPCB's online system. — [Enviraj: CTE/CTO in MP](https://oer.enviraj.com/legal/procedure-for-obtaining-cte-cto-cca-in-madhya-pradesh/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- A farmhouse colony of, say, 5–20 ha with plots sold for owners to build is very likely below 8(b) (50 ha) and, at the developer stage, below 8(a). Developer-built houses or clubhouses totalling 20,000 sq m or more of built-up area would trigger 8(a) EC from SEIAA-MP, applied for through the PARIVESH portal.
- Riverbank location does not itself trigger EC, but the NGT Narmada orders and the 300 m ban are independent constraints.
- MPPCB: an STP or common sewage discharge from the colony is the most likely MPPCB touchpoint. Confirm with the MPPCB Indore regional office whether a plotted colony under the EC thresholds needs consent to establish or operate.

### Gaps
- MPPCB-specific consent threshold for residential or building projects (any built-up area or sewage-volume cut-off) not found on an official MP source.
- SEIAA-MP fees and timelines not researched (blocked access).
- Whether MP has any state-specific EC or environmental-compliance requirement for riverbank colonies.

## Fire and road access: minimum approach road width

### Takeaway
For a Gram Panchayat colony under 2 ha, the 2014 colony rules reportedly specify a minimum right-of-way of 6 m with a 3.75 m carriageway. Larger colonies likely require wider roads; the exact figures were not retrieved. Fire-tender access standards (typically about 6 m minimum road for low-rise in Indian codes) apply mainly to high-rise or special buildings, not low-rise farmhouses.

### Cited Findings
- MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules, 2014 (search excerpt): "a minimum of 3.75 meters carriage way and 6 meter road way for a colony less than 2 hectares in area". — [LegitQuest 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2)
- MP Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012 (consolidated to Mar 2025) contains the urban layout road-width and fire-access provisions. The text could not be fetched. — [MP T&CP consolidated BVN](https://mptownplan.gov.in/act%20&%20Rules/bvn06032025E.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plan internal roads of at least 9 m right-of-way, with a 12 m entry road, for a colony above 2 ha. This is a common MP practice benchmark, not verified in the rule text. The connecting road from the public road to the colony is external development, and its cost may fall on the colonizer.
- If Jalkoti lies within the Maheshwar development plan area, Bhumi Vikas Niyam road and fire-access standards apply in addition to, or instead of, the Gram Panchayat rules.

### Gaps
- Exact Gram Panchayat road-width table for colonies above 2 ha.
- BVN rule numbers for fire approach-road width and for the threshold building height that triggers a Fire NOC.
- Whether a Fire NOC is needed for any part of a low-rise farmhouse colony (likely only for a clubhouse or assembly buildings above a size or height threshold).
