## 1. Diversion law and process under the MP Land Revenue Code 1959 as amended in 2018 (s.59, old s.172, self-assessment, portal, planning areas)

### Takeaway
Since 25 Sep 2018, diversion in MP no longer needs permission. Section 59 was substituted and the permission-based section 172 was dropped. The Bhumiswami now checks that the new use is lawful under other laws, works out the premium and one year's re-assessed land revenue himself, pays it, and gives intimation to the SDO in Form-I with a diversion sketch. The SDO has 30 days to confirm or correct the amount. Land records are updated on confirmation or after 30 days, whichever comes first. Diversion is only a revenue and land-use-assessment event. It does not override town-planning law, colony rules, or the tribal-land bars in s.165.

### Cited Findings
- The Madhya Pradesh Bhu-Rajaswa Sanhita (Bhu-Rajasva Ka Nirdharan Tatha Punarnirdharan) Niyam, 2018 were notified as No. F-2-12/2018/VII/Se.6, Bhopal, dated 28.9.2018, under s.258(2)(iii),(iv) read with ss.59 and 60. They superseded the 11 July 2014 rules and "all rules previously made on the subject". The Indian Kanoon copy says "Last Updated 13th February, 2020". — [Indian Kanoon, Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Commencement: the rules took effect from the commencement of the MP Land Revenue Code (Amendment) Act, 2018 (No. 23 of 2018), i.e. **25 September 2018**. — [Indian Kanoon, Niyam 2018, r.1(2)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- The Amendment Act 2018 substituted s.59 of the Code. — [Legitquest, MPLRC (Amendment) Act 2018](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-land-revenue-code-amendment-act-2018/1046C)
- An MP High Court order (search summary) says the old **s.172** allowed diversion only with permission and "on such conditions as the authority thought fit". After the amendment (25.09.2018), landowners self-assess and deposit the amount themselves. Pending s.172 diversion proceedings abated, and the SDO was to levy premium under new s.59. No penalty was to be imposed for one year from commencement, and that window has long expired. — [MP High Court, Gwalior, MP 1017/2020, order 16-08-2024](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2020/MP/1017/MP_1017_2020_FinalOrder_16-08-2024.pdf) (via search summary). **Superseded: s.172 permission and conversion orders, and the pre-2018 "NA order" model.**
- Rule 9: before diverting, the Bhumiswami "shall first satisfy himself that the land use ... is in compliance with the provisions of the relevant laws for the time being in force". Nothing in the rules grants permission to change land use "contrary to any law ... governing the use of such land" or in contravention of s.165(6-ee) and (7-b). — [Niyam 2018, r.9](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 10: the Bhumiswami must (a) calculate and self-assess the premium under r.7 and (b) self-assess the land revenue under r.3, then deposit the premium plus **one year's** re-assessed land revenue "in the manner directed by the State Government". — [Niyam 2018, r.10](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 11: the Bhumiswami intimates the SDO in **Form-I** with a copy of the payment receipt and gets an acknowledgment. The Form-I acknowledgment (Part II) together with the deposit receipt "shall be the proof of diversion of the land". — [Niyam 2018, r.11 and Form-I Part II](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 12: a diversion sketch, prepared to the Commissioner Land Records' directions, must be submitted with the intimation. — [Niyam 2018, r.12](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 13: the SDO checks the computation **within 30 days** and either confirms it or communicates the correct amount. Land records (other than record of rights) are updated on SDO confirmation or **30 days after intimation, whichever is earlier**. If a shortfall is communicated, the update waits for that deposit. — [Niyam 2018, r.13](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rules 14 and 15: a shortfall must be paid within **60 days** of the SDO communication, and an excess is refunded within 60 days. Late payment carries simple interest of **12% p.a. for the first 12 months and 15% p.a. after that**. — [Niyam 2018, r.14–15](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 16: the Patwari or Nagar Sarvekshak must inspect all survey numbers at least once a year and report unintimated or incorrectly intimated diversions in Form-II. The SDO then proceeds under s.59(9) and fixes premium, re-assessed land revenue, penalty and interest. — [Niyam 2018, r.16](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Section 59(9) as amended (search summary of the Indian Kanoon section text): if there is no intimation, the SDO computes premium, re-assesses land revenue and may impose a **penalty of 50% of the total amount payable**. Re-assessed land revenue is payable from the actual date of diversion, subject to a **maximum of five years**. — [Indian Kanoon, Section 59 MPLRC](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/27276173/) (summary only. Verify the exact wording.)
- Rule 17: an appeal against an SDO order needs a pre-deposit of 50% of the amount, which the appellate authority may condone. — [Niyam 2018, r.17](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Form-I mandatory enclosures: ID proof, address proof, **copy of current-year khasra**, diversion sketch, receipt of premium and land-revenue payment, and authorisation for a juristic person. Where land is co-owned, an authorisation from all co-owners or a share declaration is also required. The permitted use categories are agricultural, dwelling houses, educational, commercial, industrial (including mines and minerals), charitable, and other. — [Niyam 2018, Form-I](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Portal (secondary sources, search summary): intimation is filed online on MP Bhulekh under a "Diversion Intimation / Re-assessment (Diversion)" option, with district, tehsil, village, khasra, area and owner details. There is a separate "Diverted Land Revenue Payment" option, and WebGIS 2.0 (webgis2.mpbhulekh.gov.in) reportedly includes a re-assessment/diversion fee calculator. Diversion cases are tracked on RCMS (rcms.mp.gov.in). — [Square Yards](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/mp-bhulekh-land-records-lrid); [Legiscore](https://legiscore.in/blog/madhya-pradesh-land-records-online-check-guide); [Brokersaathi](https://brokersaathi.com/blogs/mp-bhulekh/)
- A Bhopal Samachar article (Oct 2021) covers how to apply online for diversion in MP. According to a Commissioner Land Records FAQ (29 July 2021), the portal shows a rate list after login, and the payment covers the one-time premium plus one year's land revenue. — [Bhopal Samachar, Oct 2021](https://www.bhopalsamachar.com/2021/10/mp-news_85.html); [MP Patwari – Diversion Q&A 2021 (CLR FAQ)](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Diversion_Questions_2021.pdf) (search summary)
- Small rural exemption (CLR FAQ, 29 July 2021, search summary): in rural areas, construction on agricultural land of up to **200 sq m residential** and **40 sq m commercial** is exempt from diversion. — [MP Patwari – Diversion Q&A 2021](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Diversion_Questions_2021.pdf); [mppatwari.com Q&A page](https://mppatwari.com/revenue_orders/questions_answers_about_diversion/)
- Planning area versus outside: rates depend on the local-body category "including notified development plan area". Land inside a notified development plan (planning) area of a Nagar Parishad or Municipality pays that body's rates, not the Gram Panchayat rate. — [Niyam 2018, Schedules I and II](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Inside a planning area, an MP HC order (May 2024) held that colony permissions granted only on the T&CP "opinion", without proper T&CP permission under s.13(3) of the MP Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Adhiniyam 1973 and Rule 10 of the Gram Panchayat colony rules 2014, were wrong. It also noted that a change of land use needs the Director's written permission. — [MP High Court, Indore, WP 31209/2023, order 30-May-2024](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) (search summary)

### Inferences
- In practice, for Jalkoti, the sequence would be: (1) confirm the land is not tribal-restricted, government, charnoi or submergence land; (2) confirm whether Jalkoti falls inside any notified development plan or planning area (Maheshwar town/T&CP); (3) self-assess on Bhulekh, pay, and file Form-I with the sketch and khasra; (4) the SDO Maheshwar confirms within 30 days; (5) separate T&CP and colony permissions follow (see Q6).
- Under s.59(9), diverting without intimation risks a 50% penalty plus up to 5 years' back land revenue and interest. Intimating before construction or plot sale is clearly the cheaper course.
- The 200 sq m rural exemption covers a single small house on farmland. It will not cover a colony or a large farmhouse compound, so a farmhouse of meaningful size should be diverted.

## 2. Diversion premium and annual land revenue (bhu-bhatak): formula, current rates, and a 1-acre rural example

### Takeaway
Under the 2018 rules, both the premium and the annual land revenue are **flat rupees-per-square-metre rates** that depend on the local-body category and the use. They are **not** a percentage of collector guideline value, which was the pre-2018 approach and is now superseded. For a Gram Panchayat village outside any SADA or planning area, agricultural to residential costs **Rs 1.50/sq m one-time premium plus Rs 0.50/sq m per year land revenue**. For 1 acre that is about **Rs 6,070 premium and Rs 2,020 a year**, so the first deposit is about **Rs 8,090**, subject to any post-2020 rate notification.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 7(1): when land assessed for one purpose is diverted to another, premium is payable "at the rates specified in Schedule-II". Rule 7(2): the State must notify Schedule-II rates every year for the next revenue year, and if no notification issues, the prevailing rates continue. Rule 7(3): land revenue on the diverted land is re-assessed under r.3. — [Niyam 2018, r.7](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 3: land revenue is assessed at Schedule-I rates and notified annually, with prevailing rates continuing if there is no notification. Re-assessment on diversion takes effect **from the date of diversion**. — [Niyam 2018, r.3](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- **Schedule-I, annual land revenue (Rs/sq m/year), dwelling-house use:** Municipal Corporations (including their notified development plan area) 2.00; Municipal Councils and SADAs 1.50; **Nagar Parishads (including notified development plan area) 1.00**; **Gram Panchayats (other than SADA areas) 0.50**. The commercial rates are 4.00, 3.00, 2.00 and 1.00 respectively, and the industrial rates are 3.00, 2.25, 1.50 and 0.75. Agricultural land keeps its "existing assessment". — [Niyam 2018, Schedule I](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- **Schedule-II, one-time premium (Rs/sq m), agricultural to dwelling houses:** Table 1 (Bhopal, Indore, Jabalpur, Gwalior, Ujjain Municipal Corporations) 20; Table 2 (other Municipal Corporations) 15; Table 3 (Municipal Councils and SADAs) 10; **Table 4 (Nagar Parishads including notified development plan area) 3**; **Table 5 (Gram Panchayats other than SADA areas) 1.50**. Agricultural to commercial in a Gram Panchayat is 3.00/sq m, to industrial or "other" is 2.25, and to educational is 1.50. Agricultural to charitable is 0. — [Niyam 2018, Schedule II, Tables 1–5](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Both schedules: the amount "shall not be less than ten rupees in any case and be rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees". Liability is subject to exemptions under ss.58, 58-A and 245. — [Niyam 2018, Schedule notes](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 8(1): if, after a part-diversion, the remaining area of a survey number is 100 sq m or less, the **whole survey number** is deemed diverted. Rule 8(2): adjoining land used for **ancillary purposes** (roads, parks, playgrounds, parking, utilities) is deemed diverted for the same purpose and attracts premium and land revenue. — [Niyam 2018, r.8](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 18: where diverted land is developed under applicable law and the common-utility area (roads, parks, etc.) is **transferred to the local body**, the State may exempt that area's land revenue by notification under s.58-A. — [Niyam 2018, r.18](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- Rule 19: mixed use. Where demarcated in the sketch, each area pays its own rate. Where it is not demarcated, the **highest** rate applies to the whole area. — [Niyam 2018, r.19](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- A secondary search summary, which could not be opened, says rates "have been revised periodically" and the current schedule may differ from the 2018 figures. — [1acre.in diversion guide](https://1acre.in/guides/madhya-pradesh/mp-bhulekh-na-conversion) (search summary)
- The CLR FAQ (2021) says premium and land revenue are fixed per sq m by local-body type and planning area under the 2018 rules. — [MP Patwari – Diversion Q&A 2021](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Diversion_Questions_2021.pdf); [patwarig.com FAQ](https://patwarig.com/bhumi-vyapvartan-diversion-samanya-prashn-uttar/) (search summaries)
- Context: an Oct 2025 cabinet decision fixed the reserve price for **disposal of government land** at 100% of the collector guideline value. That is a different instrument and is not the diversion premium. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/mp-news-state-cabinet-fixes-land-disposal-rate-at-full-collector-guideline-value)

### Worked examples (using 2018 Schedule rates; 1 acre = 4,046.86 sq m)
**A. Jalkoti as a Gram Panchayat village outside any notified development plan area (Table 5 / Schedule-I row 5), agricultural to dwelling houses, 1 acre:**
- Premium: 4,046.86 × Rs 1.50 = Rs 6,070.29, rounded to **Rs 6,070** (one-time)
- Annual land revenue: 4,046.86 × Rs 0.50 = Rs 2,023.43, rounded to **Rs 2,020 per year**
- First deposit under r.10(2) (premium + 1 year): **about Rs 8,090**

**B. If the land falls within a Nagar Parishad's notified development plan area (Table 4 / row 4), 1 acre residential:**
- Premium: 4,046.86 × 3 = Rs 12,140.58, rounded to **Rs 12,140**. Annual: 4,046.86 × 1.00 = **Rs 4,050**. First deposit about **Rs 16,190**.

**C. 5-acre colony in a Gram Panchayat area (20,234.3 sq m), all deemed residential including roads and parks under r.8(2):**
- Premium: 20,234.3 × 1.50 = Rs 30,351, rounded to **about Rs 30,350**. Annual: 20,234.3 × 0.50 = Rs 10,117, rounded to **about Rs 10,120**. Commercial plots, if demarcated, would attract Rs 3.00/sq m premium and Rs 1.00/sq m per year on their area.

**D. Late or no intimation (s.59(9), as summarised):** Case A amount (Rs 8,090) plus a 50% penalty (about Rs 4,045) plus back land revenue from the actual diversion date, up to 5 years (up to about Rs 10,100), plus interest.

### Inferences
- The diversion premium is small next to stamp duty and development costs. The real cost drivers for a colony are stamp duty on guideline value, colony development and EWS conditions, T&CP fees, and infrastructure.
- Because roads and parks are deemed diverted (r.8(2)), the premium is effectively paid on the gross colony area. Rule 18 offers relief on annual land revenue only if the common areas are transferred to the local body (Gram Panchayat).

### Gaps
- No post-2020 notification under r.7(2) or r.3(5) revising the Schedule I/II rates could be confirmed. The rates above may be outdated. **Confirm the current rate sheet on the MP Bhulekh diversion module (it shows the rate list after login) or with the SDM Maheshwar.**
- Whether Maheshwar is a Nagar Parishad and whether its development plan area covers Jalkoti, and whether any SADA covers the Maheshwar–Mandleshwar area, are unconfirmed. Either would move Jalkoti out of the Table 5 rates.

## 3. Collector guideline rates for Jalkoti / Maheshwar and how they drive charges

### Takeaway
No Jalkoti- or Maheshwar-specific guideline rate could be retrieved. Guideline values are published annually, effective 1 April, on mpigr.gov.in / Sampada 2.0. The 2026-27 guidelines were reported to raise values by up to about 16% on average from 1 April 2026. Under the 2018 rules, guideline value does **not** drive the diversion premium. It **does** set the minimum base for stamp duty and registration fee on every sale, both the colonizer's purchase of farmland and each plot sale.

### Cited Findings
- MP guideline values for 2025-26 took effect 01/04/2025, ran to 31/03/2026, and are published on **mpigr.gov.in**. This is shown in a district order published on a collectorate site. — [Chhatarpur district, Collector Guideline 2025-26 order](https://chhatarpur.nic.in/en/notice/order-collector-guide-line-for-valuation-of-immovable-property-year-2025-26/)
- 2024-25 guidelines rose about 7% on average (news report). — [Patrika, Bhopal](https://www.patrika.com/bhopal-news/new-collector-guidelines-in-mp-agricultural-land-to-flats-everything-become-expensive-19492918?amp=1)
- 2026-27: MP property guideline approved, with values rising by up to 16% from 1 April. Patrika reported an average 16% land-value increase and registry costs "1.5 times" higher at certain locations. — [The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/statemadhya-pradesh/madhya-pradesh-property-guideline-rate-increase-2026-11358707); [Patrika](https://www.patrika.com/bhopal-news/property-registration-to-become-1-5-times-more-expensive-in-mp-20465849); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/hi/state/mp-property-price-increase-collector-guideline-buying-property-expensive-from-april-madhya-pradesh-news-mps26021103799)
- Guidelines are prepared and revised under the MP Preparation and Revision of Market Value Guideline Rules, 2000, with revision effective 1 April. — [LatestLaws – MP Market Value Guideline Rules 2000](https://www.latestlaws.com/bare-acts/state-acts-rules/madhya-pradesh-state-laws/m-p-preparation-and-revision-of-market-value-guideline-rules-2000/)
- Stamp duty is levied on the **higher of consideration or guideline (circle) value**. — [Godrej Capital 2026 guide](https://www.godrejcapital.com/media-blog/knowledge-centre/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-madhya-pradesh)
- Market asking-price indication only: a Maheshwar listing quotes about Rs 1,000/sq ft for land near ward 9 for colony development. This is not a guideline rate. — [RealEstateIndia, Maheshwar farmland](https://www.realestateindia.com/khargone-property/farm-land-for-sale-in-maheshwar.htm)

### Inferences
- Once land is diverted and plotted, the sub-registrar will normally value plots at the **residential/plot guideline rate** for that location, which in MP guidelines is usually much higher than the agricultural per-hectare rate. So diversion indirectly raises stamp duty on subsequent plot sales.
- Guideline values may be locally higher for land fronting a main road or near Maheshwar town, so the location row in the Khargone guideline matters.

## 4. Stamp duty and registration fee in MP (2026) and how colonizer plot sales are registered

### Takeaway
Most 2026 secondary sources give **7.5% stamp duty (including local-body duty and upkar) plus 3% registration fee, about 10.5% in all**, on the higher of consideration or guideline value. There is no concession for women. One news report gives a higher urban breakdown totalling 12.5%. The rural (Janpad) component may differ, so the exact figure for Jalkoti must be taken from the Sampada 2.0 calculator. Registration is done through Sampada 2.0, which went live in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Stamp duty is 7.5% (said to include municipal/Janpad duty and upkar/cess) and the registration fee is 3% with no upper cap. No concession applies for women, joint buyers or senior citizens, and one guide notes the rate was previously 9.5%. — [Godrej Capital](https://www.godrejcapital.com/media-blog/knowledge-centre/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-madhya-pradesh); [Kotak Bank](https://www.kotak.bank.in/en/stories-in-focus/loans/home-loan/stamp-duty-property-registration-charges-in-mp.html); [Grihum Housing 2026](https://grihumhousing.com/blogs/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-in-mp) (search summaries)
- **Conflict:** The Sootr (NAREDCO concerns) gives the urban-limit breakdown as 5% stamp duty + 1% panchayat duty + 3% nigam (municipal) duty + 0.5% upkar + 3% registration fee = **12.5%**, and calls MP's stamp duty among the highest. — [The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/state/madhya-pradesh/mp-real-estate-high-stamp-duty-low-far-naredco-indore-11163574) (search summary)
- The Indian Stamp (MP Amendment) Bill 2025 was passed unanimously, and it includes a 5x fee for correction deeds. — [ETV Bharat, July 2025](https://www.etvbharat.com/hi/!state/bhopal-stamp-duty-increased-fee-for-correction-registry-indian-stamp-bill-2025-passed-assembly-madhya-pradesh-news-mps25073103027)
- Sampada (sampada.mpigr.gov.in) is run by the IGRS, Commercial Tax Department, and handles e-stamps, guideline search and registration. Sampada 2.0 recorded its first registries on day 1 of launch. — [Credit Saison guide](https://creditsaison.in/blog/mpigr-madhya-pradesh-sampada-services-guide); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/bhopal/sampada-20-three-registries-executed-in-madhya-pradesh-on-day-1-watch)
- Worked example from a secondary guide: on Rs 50,00,000, stamp duty at 7.5% is Rs 3,75,000 and the registration fee at 3% is Rs 1,50,000, a total of Rs 5,25,000. — [search summary of 2026 guides](https://www.godrejcapital.com/media-blog/knowledge-centre/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-madhya-pradesh)
- A 2026 scheme reportedly registers about 50 lakh properties (Swamitva/abadi) free of stamp duty and fees. This covers rural abadi residential properties, not colony plots. — [Prabhat Khabar](https://www.prabhatkhabar.com/national/free-property-registration-scheme-in-mp); [Navbharat Live](https://navbharatlive.com/madhya-pradesh/bhopal/mp-swamitva-yojana-2026-registration-stamp-duty-waived-1775126.html/amp)
- An Indore collector directed that development permission and T&CP layout details be recorded in khasra records, and that plot bookings be done only through RERA-registered agents. This is a local directive. — [search summary, Free Press Journal Indore](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/indore-ban-put-on-sale-of-small-plots-developed-on-agricultural-lands-to-curb-illegal-colonies)

### Worked example (illustrative only; the guideline figure is hypothetical)
- Colonizer buys 1 acre of agricultural land for Rs 40 lakh, with the guideline at, say, Rs 35 lakh. The base is the higher figure, Rs 40 lakh. At 7.5% + 3%, stamp duty is Rs 3,00,000, registration is Rs 1,20,000, and the total is **Rs 4,20,000**.
- Plot sale of 1,500 sq ft (139.35 sq m) at a hypothetical residential guideline of Rs 5,000/sq m gives a guideline value of Rs 6,96,750. If consideration is Rs 8 lakh, the base is Rs 8 lakh, giving stamp duty of Rs 60,000 and registration of Rs 24,000, a total of **Rs 84,000** (at 10.5%). Replace this with the actual Jalkoti guideline from Sampada.

### Inferences
- Each plot is conveyed by a separate sale deed from the colonizer, or from the landowner with the colonizer as a confirming party, registered on Sampada 2.0 at the Sub-Registrar office covering Maheshwar tehsil. Sub-registrars increasingly check colony permission, diversion and RERA status, and do so more strictly after illegal-colony crackdowns. That is inferred from the Indore directive and the colony rules (Q6). It is not a confirmed statewide rule.
- The registration fee in MP is uncapped, so it scales fully with value.

## 5. Restrictions and title checks: ceiling, tribal land (s.165(6)), Narmada/Maheshwar hydel submergence, government/charnoi land, non-agriculturist buyers, Khasra/B-1/naksha

### Takeaway
A non-tribal resident Indian, including a non-agriculturist, can generally buy agricultural land in MP, subject to the ceiling and the tribal bars. Tribal land in notified Scheduled Areas cannot be transferred to non-tribals (s.165(6)), and the 2018 rules expressly bar diversion in breach of s.165(6-ee)/(7-b). The Maheshwar hydel project affected 61 Narmada-bank villages, and Jalkoti's status should be checked. The project's contracts were cancelled in 2020, but acquired or submergence-marked land and R&R encumbrances may remain on record.

### Cited Findings
- Under s.165(6), tribal land in a notified Scheduled Area "shall not be transferred ... by way of sale or otherwise or as consequence of loan transaction to a non-tribal". A lease to a non-tribal is not barred, and there is no bar on tribals buying land. — [MP High Court, Jabalpur, WP 3730/2021, order 03-Mar-2021](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/jabalpur/MPHCJB/2021/WP/3730/WP_3730_2021_FinalOrder_03-Mar-2021.pdf) (search summary)
- Diversion rules: nothing permits land-use change "in contravention of sub-section (6-ee) and (7-b) of Section 165", and Form-I includes a declaration on this. — [Niyam 2018, r.9(2)(b) and Form-I declaration 3](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)

## 6. Does diversion alone allow plot sales? Interaction with colony rules and T&CP

### Takeaway
No. Diversion only changes the revenue assessment and records the use. The rules say plainly that they grant no permission against other land-use laws. Plotting and selling in a Gram Panchayat area also needs registration as a colonizer and colony development permission under the MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014, plus T&CP layout approval or permission. Inside a planning area this requires proper T&CP permission under s.13(3)/s.16 of the 1973 Act, not just an "opinion". RERA registration also applies where the project crosses the RERA thresholds. Selling plots without colony permission is an offence and creates "illegal colony" exposure.

### Cited Findings
- "Nothing in these rules shall be construed as granting the permission to change the use of land contrary to any law for the time being in force governing the use of such land." — [Niyam 2018, r.9(2)](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)
- MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014: where a colonizer "starts the development work of the colony or sells the plots without obtaining permission under rule 9 or in contravention of the terms and conditions of permission granted", the competent authority may take action. — [Legitquest – Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/d3b2) (search summary)
- The MP HC (Indore, May 2024) quashed or criticised colony permissions in a planning area granted on T&CP "opinion" instead of proper permission or approval. It relied on s.13(3) of the Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Adhiniyam 1973 and Rule 10 of the 2014 rules. — [MPHC Indore WP 31209/2023](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) (search summary)
- The older MP Gram Panchayat (Registration of Coloniser, Terms and Conditions) Rules 1999 required a 25% mortgage of developed plots until internal works were complete. Whether these rules are still in force alongside the 2014 rules is unconfirmed. — [Indian Kanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)
- For urban bodies, the MP Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules 2021 bar any development work or plot sale before colony permission. These apply if the land is inside a municipal or Nagar Parishad limit. — [Legitquest – Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)
- The MP Bhumi Vikas Rules 2012 (updated to 6 March 2025) set layout, road, open-space and setback norms. — [MP T&CP – Bhumi Vikas Rules 2012](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf)
- CAG Report No. 5 of 2025 (MP Town Planning) is relevant to enforcement of development plans and colonies. Its contents were not read. — [CAG Report 5 of 2025](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2025/Report-No-05-MP-Town-Planning-English_15-11-2025-signed-(1)-069985b0813fdf7.38091434.pdf)

### Inferences
- A typical order of steps for a Jalkoti colony: title due diligence → purchase (stamp duty on the agricultural guideline) → diversion intimation (residential, covering roads and parks) → T&CP layout permission (Khargone T&CP / Director) → colonizer registration and colony development permission from the competent authority under the 2014 rules (usually the SDM in rural areas; confirm) → RERA registration if the thresholds are met → development and mortgage of plots or bank guarantee → plot sales registered on Sampada.
- A single-owner **farmhouse**, with no plotting or sale, needs only diversion (if above the 200 sq m rural exemption) and building permission under applicable rules. A "farmhouse plots" scheme that sells subdivided parcels is functionally a colony and attracts the colony rules. Indore's ban on sale of small plots carved from agricultural land shows the enforcement direction.
