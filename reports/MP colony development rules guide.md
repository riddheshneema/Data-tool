# Jalkoti Plots Need Five Approvals Before Sale

In Madhya Pradesh, a residential colony or "farmhouse plot" scheme at Jalkoti counts as a **colony** in law as soon as land is divided into plots for sale, whether or not anything is built and whatever the plots are called. To sell legally you need five things in sequence. **First**, clean title and a written check of where the Narmada's high flood level (HFL) lies. **Second**, a Town and Country Planning (T&CP) land-use and layout permission. **Third**, a self-assessed land diversion filed with the SDM. **Fourth**, colonizer registration and colony development permission from the **SDM (Revenue), Maheshwar**, under the Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014, with **25% of developed plots mortgaged to Collector Khargone**. **Fifth**, MP RERA registration before any advertisement, video or booking. After that come development works, a completion certificate after a joint inspection with the Gram Panchayat, and handover. The government fees themselves are small: about **Rs 8 lakh in statutory fees for a 10-acre colony**, before the shelter fee, development costs and stamp duty. The binding constraint is the river. The state has told the High Court that construction within **300 m of the Narmada banks is banned**, courts have measured that line from the HFL, and a 2025 National Green Tribunal (NGT) order requires floodplains to be demarcated as no-development zones. A riverfront parcel can lose much of its saleable land, and you cannot know how much until the HFL is fixed in writing. Two other questions also decide which rulebook applies and what it costs: whether Jalkoti lies inside the Maheshwar planning area, and whether the pending "integrated" colony law (which would bring urban-style 15% EWS and heavier security) is notified mid-project. Neither could be confirmed from public sources.

**How to read this report.** This is a research synthesis for a landowner, not legal advice. Before you commit money, have an MP-qualified advocate and a T&CP-registered architect or planner verify every figure against the official gazette text. The notes behind this report had two kinds of source. Some texts were read directly: the MP Nagarpalika (Colony Development) Rules 2021, the 2018 diversion rules, the MP RERA Rules 2017, a mirror copy of the Bhumi Vikas Niyam 2012, the MPERC Supply Code, and a Times of India report on the 300 m ban. Many others, including **the 2014 rural colony rules that govern Jalkoti**, the High Court orders and all 2025–26 reform news, could only be seen through search-engine summaries because official sites were blocked during research. Facts from summaries are marked *(summary only)*.

## The law treats farmhouse plots as a colony

The legal test turns on what you do, not what you call it. Under the Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam, establishing a colony means dividing land into plots, "with or without development", for transfer to people who will build homes, and anyone doing so must first register as a colonizer ([casemine – Act text](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb46894ef23297d8c6c4) *(summary only)*). In *Satyendra Singh* (2021) the MP High Court refused to limit the penalty section, s.61-D, to registered colonizers. It said that reading would "frustrate the very purpose of the Act", so an unregistered landowner who plots and sells can be prosecuted ([MP HC WP 15953/2021](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2021/WP/15953/WP_15953_2021_FinalOrder_03-Sep-2021.pdf)). The research found **no statutory exemption for farmhouse or agricultural subdivision and no minimum-area threshold**. Districts judge by substance: a common layout, internal roads and plots marketed to many buyers all point to a colony.

The consequences are concrete. In rural areas, illegal colonization carries **up to 6 months' simple imprisonment or a minimum fine of Rs 10,000, or both**. On conviction, the colonizer's right, title and interest in the land is **forfeited to the Gram Panchayat** free of encumbrances, and building inside an illegal colony is itself an offence ([Indian Kanoon – s.61-D](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48218420/) *(summary only)*). Inside municipal limits, s.339-C was toughened in 2021 to **3–7 years plus a fine up to Rs 10 lakh**, with attachment of the offender's property ([LegitQuest – Amendment Act 22 of 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalik-vidhi-sanshodhan-adhiniyam-2021/10435)). Districts enforce this actively. In 2026, Gwalior's Collector ordered FIRs against **51 colonizers** ([AajTak](https://www.aajtak.in/madhya-pradesh/story/gwalior-collector-ruchika-chauhan-order-fir-51-illegal-colonizers-land-mafia-action-lcln-strc-2513535-2026-04-02)), Rewa acted against a 20–30 acre colony on farmland ([Rewa Riyasat](https://www.rewariyasat.com/rewa/rewa-illegal-colony-gadariya-collector-fir-action-2026-553013)), and Dewas booked 21 landowners over more than 26 ha plotted without approval ([The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/state/madhya-pradesh/mp-action-against-illegal-land-mafia-12218895)). Indore barred sale-deed registration of small plots carved from farmland and required colony plots to be booked only through RERA-registered agents ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/indore/indore-ban-put-on-sale-of-small-plots-developed-on-agricultural-lands-to-curb-illegal-colonies)).

A genuine farmhouse route does exist, but it is narrow. Bhumi Vikas Niyam (BVN) Rule 17(13) permits a farmhouse on land in agricultural use with a **minimum plot of 0.40 ha, maximum FAR 0.10, a 7.5 m height limit with a sloping roof, and access from a road at least 7.5 m wide** ([BVN 2012 mirror PDF](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)). The two available texts disagree on tree cover and setbacks. The mirror PDF requires **250 live trees per hectare and 10 m setbacks on all sides**. The 6 March 2025 consolidation, as summarised, requires **100 trees per hectare and 7.5 m on all sides** ([BVN consolidated 6 Mar 2025](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf) *(summary only)*). At FAR 0.10, a 4,000 sq m farmhouse plot allows about **400 sq m of built-up area**. Dividing a 10-acre parcel into roughly eight or nine such plots, after a 7.5 m access road, is still dividing land for sale. It therefore still triggers colonizer registration, development permission and, above the RERA threshold, RERA registration. The only lower-risk sale is a whole agricultural parcel sold as farmland, with no common layout, shared roads or "plot" marketing, and even that deserves a written legal opinion.

## Jalkoti's planning-area status decides the approval route

Everything downstream depends on one fact that no public source settles: **is Jalkoti inside the notified Maheshwar planning area?** Maheshwar has a Development Plan under the 1973 Nagar Tatha Gram Nivesh Adhiniyam with a 2021 horizon. The T&CP Khargone page lists a "Maheshwar Development Plan (Draft) 2021" ([mptownplan.gov.in Khargone](http://mptownplan.gov.in/plan_Khargone.html) *(summary only)*). Modification notices were issued on 11 Sep 2020 and 20 Jan 2023 ([mptownplan.nic.in](https://mptownplan.nic.in/) *(summary only)*). A High Court case refers to a 12 Sep 2005 order implementing the plan ([CourtKutchehry, Gangabai v State of MP](https://courtkutchehry.com/judgments/gangabai-appellant-hash-state-of-mp-respondent) *(summary only)*). A development plan can only exist for a notified section-13 planning area, so the planning area exists. Jalkoti sits in Maheshwar tehsil about **6 km from the tehsil headquarters**, listed under Kharadi Gram Panchayat in one directory, though directories conflict ([villageinfo.in](https://villageinfo.in/madhya-pradesh/khargone-west-nimar/maheshwar/jalkoti/)). Six kilometres is close enough to be inside a small-town planning ring and far enough to be outside it. Do not assume either.

The distinction matters because the High Court has drawn a hard line. In a May 2024 Indore bench order, colony permissions inside a planning area that rested only on a T&CP "opinion", rather than a proper T&CP permission under s.13(3) of the 1973 Act and Rule 10 of the 2014 rules, were held wrong. The order also noted that a change of land use needs the Director's written permission ([MP HC Indore WP 31209/2023](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) *(summary only)*). **Inside the planning area**, the sanctioned land use governs. Riverfront land is often zoned agricultural or recreational, so a residential colony there may need a plan modification. A farmhouse may be allowed in an agricultural zone. You need a full T&CP layout permission before the colony permission. **Outside the planning area**, the colony route still runs through the 2014 rules, the competent authority still obtains T&CP's opinion on the layout ([MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) *(summary only)*), and BVN layout norms are expected to apply. Whether a separate T&CP "approved layout" is legally mandatory outside a planning area is a question to put to the T&CP district office in writing.

A second geographic test sits inside the Panchayat Raj Adhiniyam. Section 61-A defines the "Gram Panchayat area" for colony purposes as land within **16 km of a Municipal Corporation, 8 km of a Municipal Council or Nagar Panchayat, 3 km of other urban areas, or 1 km of a National Highway or notified road** ([casemine – Act text](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5ed4fb46894ef23297d8c6c4) *(summary only)*). At about 6 km from Maheshwar town, Jalkoti very likely falls in that belt, which confirms the rural colony law applies. The proposed 2026 reforms target exactly these fringe villages.

The T&CP permission works as follows. You apply to the Director (the district T&CP office) under s.29, and the decision comes under s.30. If no decision is communicated within **60 days**, s.30(5) deems permission granted, excluding time you take to supply documents ([Indian Kanoon – 1973 Act](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/66016430/) *(summary only)*). Permission stays in force for **3 years** under s.33. Colonizers cannot use the shortcut: BVN Rule 23 says only individual plot holders may get instant or deemed approval, and such permission "cannot be issued to the colonizers who intend to sell the plot/flats/building" ([BVN 6 Mar 2025](https://mptownplan.gov.in/otherfiles/bvn06032025E.pdf) *(summary only)*). Online layout permission is advertised for Bhopal, Indore, Gwalior and Jabalpur, with no mention of Khargone ([emptownplan.gov.in](https://www.emptownplan.gov.in/) *(summary only)*). The only fee schedule found is Indore's undated automated-layout (ALPASS) document: **Rs 5,000/ha application fee and Rs 75,000/ha residential permission fee** (Rs 30,000/ha agricultural, Rs 1,50,000/ha commercial), paid through the state treasury portal ([ALPASS Indore PDF](http://mptownplan.gov.in/LU-panel/Indore/alpass.pdf) *(summary only)*). Treat those rates as placeholders until T&CP Khargone confirms current ones.

## The rural colony rules ask for registration, a shelter fee and 25% of plots as security

If Jalkoti is outside Maheshwar Nagar Parishad limits, which is the working assumption, the colony is governed by **ss.61-A to 61-E of the MP Panchayat Raj Avam Gram Swaraj Adhiniyam 1993** and the **MP Gram Panchayat (Development of Colonies) Rules 2014**. The rules were notified as No. 627 of 24 Dec 2014 ([indianemployees.com gazette listing](https://www.indianemployees.com/gazette-notifications/details/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014)) and came into force that day, not retrospectively ([MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) *(summary only)*). They replaced the 1999 colonizer rules, so older explainers quoting a 15% EWS hand-over and Gram Panchayat mortgages describe a **superseded** regime ([Indian Kanoon – 1999 Rules](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/179391678/)). A gazette notification of 24 Dec 2014 made the **Sub-Divisional Officer (Revenue) the competent authority**, which for Jalkoti means **SDM Maheshwar** ([Radheshwari Developers v State of MP, 2017](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/) *(summary only)*). The "colony cell" you will hear about at the Collectorate is an administrative arrangement, not a statutory body. Indore, for example, built its own CROPS portal for permissions and licences ([OneIndia Hindi](https://hindi.oneindia.com/news/indore/new-colony-development-work-online-permission-indore-madhya-pradesh-news-1252371.html)). Rural colony registration is reportedly moving to a "Vyavastha" portal ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/mp-news-registration-of-colonies-in-rural-areas-through-vyavastha-portal)).

The process has four parts. **Registration** comes first: an application in Form-1 under Rule 3 ([Radheshwari, 2017](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/31141563/) *(summary only)*), with a fee reported as **Rs 50,000 and Rs 10,000 for renewal**, paid into the Zila Panchayat shelter fund ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) *(summary only)*). One explainer gives Rs 5,000/Rs 2,000, which probably reflects the 1999 rules ([LinkedIn explainer](https://www-linkedin-com.translate.goog/pulse/registration-colonizer-colonies-madhya-pradesh-harsh-kushwaha?_x_tr_sl=en&_x_tr_tl=hi&_x_tr_hl=hi&_x_tr_pto=tc)). **Development permission** follows, at a fee of **Rs 10,000 per hectare**, also paid into the Zila Panchayat shelter fund. An application without the fee receipt or complete documents must be rejected. The SDM must decide **within 60 days**, and a separate clause deems permission granted if no order is passed within 15 days of a reminder letter from the applicant ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) *(summary only)*). **Security** is a mortgage of **25% of the developed plots, or 25% of dwelling units, with the District Collector** ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/d3b2) *(summary only)*). **EWS and shelter obligations** come last. A High Court summary describes reserving **6% of saleable area (3% EWS + 3% LIG)**, inside the colony for colonies under 2 ha and optionally within a 2 km radius for larger ones ([MP HC Indore 2024 order](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/indore/MPHCIND/2023/WP/31209/WP_31209_2023_FinalOrder_30-May-2024.pdf) *(summary only)*). There is also a **shelter fee pegged to the Collector guideline rate on the date of application** (Rule 14(2)), plus an **additional shelter fee for maintaining external roads, drainage and sewage**, which you hand to the Gram Panchayat ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) *(summary only)*). The rural shelter-fee percentage was not found. The EWS income criterion was set at **Rs 1,00,000 per family per year** ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) *(summary only)*). Allotting EWS/LIG plots to ineligible buyers can cost you your registration.

Completion under the rural rules works like this. You report completion to the SDM and the Gram Panchayat. The SDM inspects **jointly with Gram Panchayat representatives within 30 days**. If works and EWS/LIG allotments check out, the SDM issues a **completion certificate in Form-7** and releases the mortgaged plots by order in **Form-8**, phase by phase where permission was phased ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/d3b2) *(summary only)*). No staged release (for example 50% of plots at 50% of works) was found in the rural text. Plan cash flow on the assumption that the 25% stays locked until each phase is fully complete. The rural time limit for works is also unconfirmed. One secondary summary says 3 years.

**Verified from the rule text (added after the main research).** A July 2026 MP High Court judgment reproduces Rules 15, 16 and 20 of the 2014 rules verbatim, which confirms these figures directly ([Gopal v State of MP, M.Cr.C. 28745/2025, 10 Jul 2026, 2026:MPHC-IND:18522](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/104094571/)):
- **Rule 16:** 25% of the developed plots, or 25% of the dwelling units, are mortgaged with the District Collector, with the mortgage deed at the colonizer's cost.
- **Rule 20:** the joint inspection with the Gram Panchayat happens within 30 days of the completion report. The completion certificate is issued in Form-7 and the mortgaged plots are released in Form-8, but only once external and internal works are complete **and** the EWS/LIG plots have been developed and allotted.
- **Rule 15:** you must invite applications for the EWS/LIG plots from MP-domiciled families with no house or plot in MP. The applicants file a Form-10 declaration, and the Collector checks the list within 30 days. If the Collector doesn't decide in that time, you may sell to the applicants on your list. Allotting to ineligible buyers can cost you your registration.

The same judgment shows the risk in practice. In Indore, the partners of a colonizer had a criminal FIR filed against them for selling mortgaged plots before the completion certificate was issued. The court quashed it, saying the 2014 rules are regulatory and carry no penal clause of their own, but it ordered authorities to verify every rule strictly before issuing completion certificates. The certificates in that case took about two years to arrive (applied June 2023, issued May–June 2025), so **build a completion-certificate delay into the cash-flow plan**. The judgment also names the Collector (Indore) as the authority that issued the completion certificates, so whether SDM Maheshwar or Collector Khargone handles your file must be confirmed locally.

The urban rules apply only if the site is, or later becomes, part of Maheshwar Nagar Parishad. Rule 8 of the 2021 urban rules requires a merged rural colony to re-apply, with fees already paid to the Gram Panchayat adjusted ([LegitQuest – Nagarpalika Colony Rules 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)). The urban rules are heavier, and they are also the template for the pending integrated law, so it is worth seeing how they compare.

| Obligation | Rural: 2014 GP rules (SDM Maheshwar) | Urban: 2021 Nagarpalika rules (Collector for a Nagar Parishad) |
|---|---|---|
| Colonizer registration | Rs 50,000; renewal Rs 10,000 *(summary only)* | Rs 50,000; renewal Rs 25,000; 5-year validity; statewide; online; decided in 30 days |
| Development permission fee | Rs 10,000/ha | 0.5% of guideline market value of the diverted land |
| Supervision fee | Not found | 2% of internal development cost |
| EWS/LIG | 6% of saleable area, plus shelter fee and additional shelter fee *(summary only)* | 15% of plots or units, or a shelter fee of 5% × plot area × guideline value; plus a 1% additional shelter fee |
| Security | 25% of developed plots mortgaged to the Collector | Plots worth 125% of internal development cost, or an equal bank guarantee |
| Decision time | 60 days; deemed after a 15-day reminder *(summary only)* | Decided in 45 days; deemed 30 days after written notice |
| Works deadline | Unconfirmed (3 years reported) | 5 years, plus 1 year for 20% of the permission fee |
| Mortgage release | Form-8 after Form-7 completion | 50% / 25% / 25% at 50% / 75% / 100% of works |
| Handover | External roads, drains and sewage to the Gram Panchayat | Deemed transfer to the Resident Welfare Association on the completion certificate |

The urban figures come from the full 2021 text ([LegitQuest – Nagarpalika Colony Rules 2021](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-nagarpalika-colony-development-rules-2021/C65B)). The 3-to-5-year change in the works deadline was reported separately ([Patrika Betul](https://www.patrika.com/betul-news/colonizers-will-get-five-years-permission-for-colony-development-7294257)).

The pending reform is the biggest regulatory risk. The Urban Administration department has drafted a "**Madhya Pradesh Colony Integration Act, 2026**". It would bring villages within **16 km of municipal limits** under urban rules with a single licence and raise maximum jail from 7 to 10 years ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/bhopal/noose-on-illegal-colonies-to-tighten-as-uniform-law-to-crack-down-for-16-km-radius-from-municipal-limits)). Draft "Integrated Colony Development Rules 2026" would extend the 2021 urban rules to Gram Panchayat areas ([ETV Bharat, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.etvbharat.com/hi/state/madhya-pradesh-colony-act-2026-draft-prepared-colonizer-to-do-development-in-5-years-mandatory-mps26062603902)). Reports say District Collectors would approve rural colonies, with deemed approval after **75 days** ([Indore Talk](https://indoretalk.com/madhya-pradesh-plans-major-overhaul-of-colony-development-rules-2/)), and that 15% EWS/LIG would apply ([Star Samachar](https://starsamachar.com/Madhya-Pradesh:-Government-will-bring-Colony-Act---15-percent-houses-will-be-reserved-for-the-poor)). A bill was listed for the July 2026 monsoon session ([Haribhoomi](https://www.haribhoomi.com/state-local/madhya-pradesh/news/mp-monsoon-session-2026-ucc-10-bills-illegal-colony-law-10-years-jail-103739)). The latest report says the stricter law has been stuck for about two years after objections from a committee of senior secretaries ([Patrika Bhopal](https://www.patrika.com/bhopal-news/over-8000-illegal-colonies-in-mp-building-a-house-will-result-in-a-10-year-prison-sentence-20984844)). The prudent reading is that the 2014 rules govern today, but budget for a switch to 15% EWS and 125% security if the project will still be under way in 2027–28.

## Diversion costs about Rs 8,000 per acre but permits nothing else

Diversion changed fundamentally on **25 September 2018**. The amended s.59 of the MP Land Revenue Code and the 2018 assessment rules replaced the old permission-based s.172 "NA order" with **self-assessment and intimation** ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/); [MP HC Gwalior MP 1017/2020](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/gwalior/MPHCGWL/2020/MP/1017/MP_1017_2020_FinalOrder_16-08-2024.pdf) *(summary only)*). The steps are as follows. You first satisfy yourself that the new use is lawful under other laws (Rule 9). You compute the premium and one year's re-assessed land revenue and pay both (Rule 10). You file **Form-I with the SDO**, attaching ID, the current-year khasra, a diversion sketch and the payment receipt (Rules 11–12). The SDO must confirm or correct the amount **within 30 days**, and land records update on confirmation or after 30 days, whichever is earlier (Rule 13). A shortfall is payable within 60 days, with interest of **12% a year for the first 12 months and 15% after that** (Rules 14–15) ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)). Filing is reportedly done on MP Bhulekh under a "Diversion Intimation / Re-assessment" option, and the rate list appears after login ([Square Yards](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/mp-bhulekh-land-records-lrid); [MP Patwari – CLR FAQ 2021](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Diversion_Questions_2021.pdf) *(summary only)*).

Under the 2018 rules the charges are **flat rupees per square metre**, set by local-body category, not by guideline value. For a Gram Panchayat area outside any SADA, agricultural to dwelling-house use costs **Rs 1.50/sq m as a one-time premium and Rs 0.50/sq m a year in land revenue**. Inside a Nagar Parishad's notified development plan area the rates double to **Rs 3 and Rs 1** ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018, Schedules I–II](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)). Two rules raise the bill for a colony. Under Rule 8(2), roads, parks and utilities serving the scheme are **deemed diverted**, so you pay on the gross area. Under Rule 19, mixed use that is not demarcated in the sketch pays the **highest** applicable rate on the whole area, so mark any commercial plots separately at Rs 3/sq m premium. Rule 18 lets the State exempt annual land revenue on common areas transferred to the local body ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)). For **one acre in a Gram Panchayat** that works out to a premium of 4,046.86 × 1.50 = **Rs 6,070**, plus **Rs 2,020 a year**, for a first deposit of about **Rs 8,090**. Inside a Nagar Parishad plan area the first deposit is about **Rs 16,190**. Diverting without intimation invites a **50% penalty**, back land revenue for up to **five years** and interest ([Indian Kanoon – s.59](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/27276173/) *(summary only)*). The rates may have been revised after 2020 ([1acre.in](https://1acre.in/guides/madhya-pradesh/mp-bhulekh-na-conversion) *(summary only)*), so confirm on the Bhulekh rate sheet. The **200 sq m residential exemption** for rural construction on farmland ([MP Patwari – CLR FAQ 2021](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Diversion_Questions_2021.pdf) *(summary only)*) covers one small house. It does not cover a colony or a sizeable farmhouse.

Diversion confers no right to plot. Rule 9(2) states that "nothing in these rules shall be construed as granting the permission to change the use of land contrary to any law" ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)). The real land costs are therefore elsewhere. Stamp duty is commonly given as **7.5% plus a 3% registration fee (10.5% in total)** on the higher of consideration or guideline value, with no concession for women ([Godrej Capital](https://www.godrejcapital.com/media-blog/knowledge-centre/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-madhya-pradesh) *(summary only)*). One report breaks the urban rate down to **12.5%** ([The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/state/madhya-pradesh/mp-real-estate-high-stamp-duty-low-far-naredco-indore-11163574) *(summary only)*), so take the Jalkoti figure from the Sampada 2.0 calculator. Guideline values for 2026-27 rose by **up to about 16%** from 1 April 2026 ([The Sootr](https://thesootr.com/statemadhya-pradesh/madhya-pradesh-property-guideline-rate-increase-2026-11358707)). Once land is diverted and plotted, sub-registrars normally value each sale at the residential plot rate, which is far higher than the agricultural rate.

Title checks come before any of this. Tribal land in a notified Scheduled Area cannot be sold to a non-tribal under s.165(6) ([MP HC WP 3730/2021](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/jabalpur/MPHCJB/2021/WP/3730/WP_3730_2021_FinalOrder_03-Mar-2021.pdf) *(summary only)*), and Form-I includes a declaration on s.165(6-ee) and (7-b) ([Indian Kanoon – Niyam 2018](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/40566298/)). Khargone has a large tribal population, so check the seller's caste entry and the Scheduled Area status of Maheshwar tehsil. The **Maheshwar hydel project** affected **61 Narmada-bank villages**, 21 to be fully submerged and 40 losing farmland. The state cancelled the project's contracts in 2020 ([Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/madhya-pradesh-govt-cancels-all-maheshwar-hydroelectric-contracts-85235)), but acquisition or submergence entries can still sit on the khasra. Also check the khasra (P-II), B-1, bhu-naksha, RCMS case search and Sampada encumbrance records for government, charnoi (grazing), patta or ceiling-surplus status ([Legiscore](https://legiscore.in/blog/madhya-pradesh-land-records-online-check-guide)).

## Bhumi Vikas Niyam sets 10% open space and ties road width to road length

The BVN 2012 layout rules shape how much of the land you can sell. The figures below come from the mirror PDF, whose consolidation date is unknown, so check them against the official 6 March 2025 consolidation and the June 2026 amendments, which are known only by title. Rule 47(2) requires **community open space of at least 10% of the layout area**. Rule 38 Table 3 sets residential road widths by length: **7.5 m up to 150 m, 9 m up to 250 m, 12 m up to 400 m, 18 m up to 1,000 m and 24 m beyond**, and no plot may be developed unless it fronts a public street **at least 7.5 m wide**. Rule 43 allows cul-de-sacs of 150–275 m only on straight residential roads, with a turning space of at least 121 sq m. Rule 53 sets minimum plot types: **detached above 225 sq m with frontage over 12 m; semi-detached 125–225 sq m with 8–12 m frontage; row-type 50–225 sq m with 4.5–12 m frontage**. Rule 56 sets front setbacks of 3 m on streets up to 9 m wide, 3.6 m up to 12 m and 4.5 m up to 18 m. Rear setbacks run from nil (up to 40 sq m) through 1.5 m and 2.5 m to 3 m above 225 sq m, and detached houses need 3 m on both sides. Residential **FAR is 1.25** (Rule 61, Table 8). Community facilities are scaled to population rather than set as a percentage: one nursery and 10 convenience shops per 4,000 people, and a primary school site of 0.4–0.6 ha per 4,000 ([BVN 2012 mirror PDF](https://www.citygas.mp.gov.in/cgdpolicy/bhoomi_vikas_niyam-t&cp.pdf)). A colony of 5–20 acres houses roughly 300–1,600 people, so it needs only a small shop or nursery site.

Two conflicts need settling at T&CP. First, a summary of the 2014 rural rules quotes **a 6 m right-of-way with a 3.75 m carriageway for colonies under 2 ha** ([LegitQuest – 2014 Rules](https://www.legitquest.com/act/madhya-pradesh-gram-panchayat-development-of-colonies-rules-2014/D3B2) *(summary only)*), which is narrower than BVN's 7.5 m minimum. Design to 7.5 m, with 9 m loops and a 12 m spine wherever a straight run exceeds 250 m, so the layout satisfies both. Second, BVN's general water-body setbacks cited in a High Court order, **9 m from water bodies and 15 m from rivers** ([MP HC WA 318/2018](https://mphc.gov.in/upload/jabalpur/MPHCJB/2018/WA/318/WA_318_2018_FinalOrder_05-Apr-2023.pdf)), are much smaller than the Narmada-specific line discussed next. Do not design to 15 m.

Recent changes are mostly urban and do not reach a Jalkoti colony. Premium FAR is capped at 25% of base FAR ([TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/40256/govt-of-mp-issued-amendments-in-the-madhya-pradesh-bhumi-vikas-rules-2/)). Green buildings get a 3–5% FAR bonus from December 2025 ([TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50844/govt-of-madhya-pradesh-issued-amendments-to-the-madhya-pradesh-bhumi-v/)). The **Integrated Township Policy 2025** needs at least **10 ha** within municipal or planning-area limits ([Township Policy PDF](https://mppatwari.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Approved-Integrated-Township-Policy-22.02.2025.pdf)), so a 5–20 acre (2–8 ha) project falls outside it and proceeds as an ordinary colony.

## The Narmada's 300-metre line can remove the riverfront from sale

The river is the constraint that can turn a viable colony into an unviable one. Answering a PIL, the state government told the MP High Court that "there can be no construction up to a distance of 300 metre from the banks of river Narmada", citing a T&CP notification. A division bench ordered **removal of all structures within 300 m**, with removals filmed and an amicus curiae appointed ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bhopal/constructions-upto-300-metre-from-narmada-bank-banned/articleshow/94442983.cms)). In a Jabalpur matter the Court spoke of construction "within 300 meters of **high flood level**" ([India Legal](https://indialegallive.com/top-news-of-the-day/news/narmada-bank-encroachment-jabalpur-tilwaraghat/)). The HFL can lie well inland of the normal channel on the flat Nimar banks, so measuring from the HFL is the conservative and correct working assumption. The NGT added a second layer on 1 September 2025. It ordered **floodplains demarcated as no-development zones on a 25-year flood basis**, removal of encroachments and a halt to untreated sewage discharge, under a committee monitored by the Chief Secretary ([SupremeToday digest](https://supremetoday.ai/ngt-issues-sweeping-directives-to-stop-narmada-river-pollution-mandates-demarcation-of-no-development-floodplain-zones-INDNGT00000029736) *(summary only)*). It also banned plastic within 100 m of the river ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/local/mp/news/ngt-plastic-ban-100m-narmada-river-pollution-action-report-137085816.html)) and reiterated strict compliance in January 2026 ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/daily-court-digest-major-environment-orders-january-29-2026)).

The research could not find the number and date of the T&CP notification, or whether it reaches rural Gram Panchayat land outside a planning area. That is the single most important open question for a riverbank site. Until the Water Resources Department or NVDA certifies the HFL and floodplain line for your khasra in writing, plan **every plot and building beyond 300 m from the HFL**. Use the strip in between for farming, orchards or the open-space requirement, provided T&CP accepts riverside open space towards the 10%. On a narrow riverfront parcel this can take out a third or more of the land. That figure is an inference that depends entirely on the site's geometry.

Other environmental approvals are lighter. Under the EIA Notification 2006, building projects need prior environmental clearance from SEIAA-MP only at **20,000 sq m or more of built-up area** (item 8(a)), and townships only at **50 ha or more** (item 8(b)) ([NGT affidavit OA 36/2024](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Counter%20Affidavit%20by%20R-1%20in%20OA%20No.36-2024%20(page%20nos.994-1047).pdf)). A plotted colony of 2–8 ha where buyers build their own houses falls below both thresholds. Developer-built houses or a clubhouse would count towards the 20,000 sq m. MPPCB consent to establish or operate is most likely to be triggered by a common STP. No official MP threshold was found, so ask the Indore regional office. For heritage, the only centrally protected monuments in Khargone district are at **Un** ([ASI Bhopal monument list](https://asibhopal.nic.in/monument/all_monument4.html)), so the 100 m and 300 m zones of the AMASR Act very likely do not reach Jalkoti. State protection of Maheshwar's fort and ghats is unconfirmed. The **Ahilya Lok** project, about 15 acres and Rs 110 crore on Maheshwar's riverfront ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/ahilya-lok-project)), will raise demand and could bring special-area regulation.

## Developers pay for the transformer, the pipes and the sewage

The colonizer pays for the utilities. MP Electricity Supply Code clause 4.41 says the developer of a housing colony "shall bear the cost of extension including the cost of 11 kV line, distribution transformer and LT lines". If the colony load exceeds **2,150 kW**, or the existing 33/11 kV substation cannot be augmented, the developer also bears the 33 kV line and the substation ([MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)). Clause 4.42 fixes the deemed load per plot by area: 1,000–1,500 sq ft is 3 kW, 1,500–2,000 sq ft is 4 kW, 2,400–3,000 sq ft is 7 kW, and an EWS plot is 1 kW. On those figures, a 10-acre colony of about 155 plots of 150 sq m plus EWS plots carries a deemed load of roughly **630 kW**, well under the substation threshold. You may build the works through an 'A'-class licensed contractor to discom-approved estimates and pay a supervision charge (clause 4.11) ([MPERC Supply Code](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64f4bcca0f194dbb46ece5cb5a6a0553.pdf)). The per-kW electrification rate must be published by the discom under the 2022 cost-recovery regulations ([MPERC Gazette 31 May 2022](https://mperc.in/uploads/regulation_document/64140ba9cef8abeaa895ae894404f764.pdf)). No current West Discom figure was found, so get the estimate from the Maheshwar or Mandleshwar division. Ask for supply from a non-agricultural feeder, because a farm feeder may run on rationed hours.

Water and sewage follow the colony rules. The rural rules list water pipelines, drainage, sewerage and open-space development among the colonizer's works, and a May 2026 High Court matter refers to "external development works like laying water pipelines etc, as mandated under Rule 12" ([LiveLaw PDF, Bharat Enterprises v State of MP](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/05/12/bharat-enterprises-v-state-of-madhya-pradesh-673503.pdf)). For groundwater, the CGWA 2020 guidelines require an NOC for residential apartments and group housing where government supply falls short, and make an STP mandatory for new apartments and group housing whose groundwater demand exceeds **20 KLD** ([CGWA consolidated guidelines](https://cgwa-noc.gov.in/landingpage/Guidlines/ConsolidateGuidline.pdf)). Individual domestic users and farmers are exempt ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/farmers-exempted-from-taking-noc-to-extract-groundwater-893414)). A common colony borewell probably needs an NOC, depending on Maheshwar block's groundwater category. Given the NGT's focus on untreated discharge into the Narmada, assume the authorities will require a decentralised STP, or at minimum septic tanks and soak pits set well back from the river, and that nothing can discharge to the river untreated.

## MP RERA bars every advertisement until registration

A plotted colony is a "real estate project" because the Act includes "the development of land into plots for the purpose of selling" ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/rera-applicability-plots-farmhouses.html); [RERA Act, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2158)). A farmhouse scheme with shared roads, power and water is treated the same way ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/rera-applicability-plots-farmhouses.html)). The s.3(2)(a) exemption covers land of **500 sq m or less, or eight or fewer units across all phases**. Commentators disagree on whether a project must meet one limit or both to be exempt ([Argus Partners](https://argus-p.com/papers-publications/thought-paper/rera-regime-the-exemption-conundrum/)), so get MP RERA's view in writing before relying on an "eight farmhouse plots" structure. Section 3(1) applies the bar "in any planning area". That gives a promoter outside a planning area an argument against registration, but the MP Rules apply statewide and ask for rural revenue documents such as an SDO (Revenue) NOC ([MP RERA Rules 2017](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/)). Register.

Registration uses Form A and Form B, with title proof or a Tehsildar non-encumbrance certificate, three years of financials, the approvals (in practice the T&CP layout, the diversion record and the colony permission), the layout and the development-works plan. The fee for plotted residential projects is **Rs 10 × permissible FAR per sq m of plot area, excluding roads and green areas**. The Authority must decide within **30 working days**, but defect-rectification time and inquiry time are excluded. Registration runs for the completion period you declare. An extension (Form E) costs a fee equal to the original fee and is capped at one year ([MP RERA Rules 2017](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/)). In practice, reports say registration takes about **four months in MP** ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/indore/indore-real-estate-regulatory-authority-registration-takes-15-days-in-rajasthan-4-months-in-mp)), and an October 2025 High Court judgment criticised the use of Rule 6 to suspend the statutory clock ([LatestLaws](https://www.latestlaws.com/judgements/madhya-pradesh-high-court/2025/october/2025-latest-caselaw-10044-mp) *(summary only)*).

Once registered, the money rules are strict. **70% of buyer collections** must go to a separate scheduled-bank account within 15 days and may be withdrawn only in proportion to completion, on engineer, architect and CA certificates, with an annual CA audit ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016-2/)). MP requires quarterly updates on the project page within 10 working days of each quarter ([MP RERA Rules 2017](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/)). Marketing videos, drone walkthroughs, WhatsApp broadcasts and "EOI" collections are all "advertising" and must wait for the Form C certificate. Afterwards, every advertisement must show the registration number and the Authority's website ([MP RERA Rules r.6(3)](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/)). Agents must be RERA-registered (Rs 10,000 for an individual, Rs 50,000 for a firm), and plot areas must be disclosed. The sale agreement must follow the MP model at Annexure A. You may take **no more than 10% of the price** before a registered agreement, and defects reported within **five years** of possession must be fixed free within 30 days ([MP RERA Rules 2017](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/); [RERA Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2158)). Non-registration carries a penalty of **up to 10% of estimated project cost**, and continued violation adds up to three years' imprisonment ([RERA Act s.59](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2158)). MP RERA's Rs 2.27 crore penalty on an unregistered project was restored by the Supreme Court ([LiveLawBiz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/rera/supreme-court-sets-aside-high-court-order-quashing-227-crore-rera-penalty-524082) *(summary only)*). At completion, RERA relies on the local authority's certificate, which for Jalkoti means the SDM's Form-7. You upload it, enable an allottees' association (at least two-thirds of allottees, per [MP RERA Rules r.2(e)](https://ibclaw.in/madhya-pradesh-real-estate-regulation-and-development-rules-2017/)) and execute conveyance deeds.

## Conclusion

For a riverbank landowner the usual economics are inverted. The fees that sound intimidating, such as T&CP permission, colony permission, diversion and RERA, add up to roughly Rs 80,000 per acre. What decides the project is a set of lines on a map and in a gazette: the HFL-based 300 m setback, the planning-area boundary, and whether the integrated colony law lands before you finish. None of these can be settled by paying a fee, and two of them can only be settled by an official writing them down. So the order of work matters more than speed. Get the HFL, the land-use certificate and the planning-area answer in writing before you draw a layout, and draw the layout before you announce anything.

The second point is that structure is not a loophole. The farmhouse label, phasing, or staying at eight plots changes your fees and your RERA position, but courts and Collectors judge colonization by substance. MP's enforcement trend runs firmly towards prosecuting landowners as well as dealers. A project designed to the stricter urban standard (15% EWS or shelter fee, bank-guarantee-ready security, 7.5 m minimum roads, an STP set back from the river) costs somewhat more today but would survive the reform that is coming. A project designed to the minimum of the 2014 rules is a bet that the reform stays stalled.
