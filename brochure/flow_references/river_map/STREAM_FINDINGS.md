# Jalkoti stream — measured findings

**Method.** Centreline from OpenStreetMap (two unnamed ways tagged `waterway=river`).
Width measured from Esri World Imagery at zoom 18 (0.553 m/pixel, post-monsoon capture),
sampled every 20 m perpendicular to the centreline, taking the contiguous non-vegetated
channel (open water + sand bed). 36 stations near the colony returned a clear reading;
stations under closed tree canopy are reported as "no reading", not as zero.

## Result

| | metres | feet |
|---|---|---|
| Lower quartile | 5.9 | 19 |
| **Median** | **9.8** | **32** |
| Upper quartile | 17.5 | 57 |
| Maximum (bend pool) | 50.5 | 166 |

Accuracy about ±2 m. This is the channel at the date of that imagery, **not** the legal
high-water line.

## Conclusion

1. It is a **stream, not a river**. Nothing here supports calling it a river, and it is
   not the Narmada.
2. The owner's figure of **20–30 ft is right** for a typical reach.
3. The repo brief (`brochure/Flow_Image_Brief.md`) says "about 10 ft (3 m)" — **that is too
   narrow and should be corrected**.
4. It is **seasonal**. Google's 12 Mar 2025 imagery (dry season) shows far less water than
   Esri's post-monsoon capture over the same ground. Wide bed, variable flow.
5. Safe brochure wording: *"a seasonal stream along the north-west edge, with a 15 m
   landscaped garden between it and the plots."* Never "riverside" or "Narmada".

## If you want it to hold water all year

A low check dam at a narrow throat would impound a permanent pool through April–May, which
is when the stream currently looks worst and when buyers visit. Three candidate throats
(4–6 m wide, with 18 m+ water beside them) are marked on `river_width_map.png`.

These are candidates from imagery only. Siting a weir needs levels and a bed survey, plus
Water Resources permission. The T&CP test already lists the high-water-level survey as an
action item, so do both together.
